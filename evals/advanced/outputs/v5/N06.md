The confusing part is that **“95%” describes how reliably the method works across repeated samples**.

Imagine a batch of 10,000 packages. Their true average weight is one particular number, but you do not know it. You weigh a random sample and calculate a confidence interval—say, **98–102 grams**.

Now imagine repeating the sampling process many times:

- Each new sample gives you a new range.
- The batch’s true average stays the same.
- With a valid 95% confidence method, about 95% of those ranges would contain that true average.

So the **ranges change; the number they are trying to capture stays put**.

For your particular range, 98–102 grams, the true average either falls inside it or does not. You remain uncertain which. But the 95% guarantee describes the method’s success rate across repeated samples; it does not assign a 95% probability to this particular range containing the average.

You can read “95% confident” as: **“I used a method that produces ranges containing the true value 95% of the time, when its assumptions hold.”**
