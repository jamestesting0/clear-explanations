Adding A increases one upstream component; it does not ensure that Q reaches a compartment where it can produce light. The experiments support several parts of that explanation, but they do not establish the complete working model or identify a single bottleneck.

### Separate the four things being measured

Under the laboratory's working model, light requires a connected sequence: E uses charged C to make Q; R carries Q across an intact membrane; and L consumes Q inside the compartment when local acidity is below the threshold. A increases E after a delay, without directly supplying the other conditions. This is the proposed mechanism against which to interpret the experiments, not an independently proved account of every step. [B01-S1]

Four distinctions matter:

- **Production:** More E protein means more enzyme is present, not necessarily that it converts P faster. Its activity also depends on conditions such as charged C.
- **Location:** Whole-cell Q includes Q on both sides of the membrane. A large total does not show that Q has reached L.
- **Consumption:** Q is an intermediate that can be used up. Its measured amount reflects accumulation after production and consumption, rather than measuring production alone.
- **Final readout:** Light reports a downstream result. An increase in E or Q does not by itself establish that every required step is working. [B01-S1]

### What the cultures show

At minute twenty, A-treated cultures had four times the E abundance and substantially more whole-cell Q than untreated cultures, but light changed only from 8 to 9. Adding the cofactor-charging treatment increased Q further, from 38 to 75, while light reached 10. These observations fit an explanation in which upstream supply increases but some downstream condition still restricts light. They do not directly measure E's reaction rate or locate the accumulated Q. The results are means from three cultures without uncertainty estimates, so the small light differences cannot confidently be distinguished from experimental variation. [B01-S2]

After M, Q fell from 38 to 17 while light rose from 9 to 31. This does **not** prove that E became less active. Better delivery of Q could let L consume it faster, leaving less Q present even while more light-producing product is made. Simply moving Q across the membrane would not lower the whole-cell total, because that assay counts both locations. The fall indicates a net decrease in the measured Q pool; relocation alone cannot explain that decrease. [B01-S1; B01-S2]

M's separate transport assay strengthens the transport explanation. However, that assay did not measure local acidity, so it does not establish that transport was M's only relevant effect. Unchanged E abundance also does not guarantee unchanged E activity. Increased consumption, reduced production, or both remain possible contributors to the falling Q pool. [B01-S2]

### Why the apparently decisive experiments remain ambiguous

Deleting R eliminated light, but the deletion also damaged the membrane. The experiment therefore shows that this combined disruption prevents light under the tested conditions; it does not isolate R's transport function as the cause. Failure of Q supplied outside the compartment to rescue those cultures cannot distinguish failed entry from consequences of membrane damage. [B01-S3]

Supplying Q inside intact isolated compartments produced light below the acidity threshold without A, and little light above it. This supports a downstream capacity to use internally supplied Q and shows that A is unnecessary for that result in this preparation. It also gives a direct reason to keep acidity in the explanation. But isolation removes soluble proteins and supplies charged C, so the experiment does not reproduce all the requirements of a whole cell or independently establish L as the sole responsible enzyme. [B01-S3]

Similarly, restoring R would be an ambiguous rescue if it also repaired the membrane: restored light could follow from restored transport, restored integrity, or both. The supplier's satisfaction score adds no evidence about any of these mechanisms. [B01-S3; B01-S4]

### A small set of experiments to separate the explanations

1. **Measure Q production and use over time, with its location resolved.** Compare A alone, A plus cofactor charging, and A plus M. Measure P-to-Q conversion directly, charged C, Q inside and outside the compartment, and light. If production remains steady while Q is consumed faster and light rises after M, that supports increased downstream use. If P-to-Q conversion falls, reduced production contributes. Location measurements would show whether A mainly builds an outside pool that cannot reach L.

2. **Vary transport and acidity separately while checking membrane integrity.** Measure transport and local acidity in the same preparations, then compare increased transport at controlled acidity with lowered acidity at comparable transport. More light after improved transport at fixed permissive acidity supports a transport constraint. More light after lowering acidity while transport is unchanged supports an acidity constraint. If both changes are required, both stages limit output; neither can be dismissed as irrelevant.

3. **Separate R restoration from membrane repair.** Test the proposed R rescue while measuring both transport and integrity, and, if feasible, compare it with an independent membrane repair. If integrity is restored but transport and light still require R, that strengthens the specific transport explanation. If repair restores light without R, R is not indispensable under those rescue conditions. A rescue that restores everything together would leave the original ambiguity unresolved. Repeat these comparisons with uncertainty estimates so that apparent differences can be judged against culture-to-culture variation.
