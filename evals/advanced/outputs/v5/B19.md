The starting signal can remain causally important after it disappears: it starts a process that other components then maintain. A lingering fluorescent signal is a third, separate thing—it can remain after the activity that produced it has stopped.

### Starting the switch and keeping it active

In the proposed model, S activates A that is already present in an inactive form. Active A promotes production of B, and B helps keep A active. This reinforcing loop is **positive feedback**: activity helps create the conditions that sustain more activity. It does not create its components from nothing or guarantee that they will last forever. [B19-S1]

The direct A measurements support a distinction between initiation and maintenance:

- With a ten-minute S pulse, A became active and stayed highly active for two hours after S was removed. S therefore did not have to remain present throughout that observed maintenance period.
- Without S, the standard resting devices stayed inactive during the tested interval. S was required to start these devices under the tested preparation and interventions; other starting routes have not all been ruled out.
- Without B, S still produced an early A response, but A returned to baseline after S removal. B was therefore dispensable for that early response but needed for the sustained response in this experiment. The measured controls make altered initial A abundance or an altered S sensor poor explanations for the difference. [B19-S2]

Because B was deleted **before** stimulation, this experiment does not completely distinguish B's role in establishing the persistent state from its continuing role after that state exists. It supports the proposed feedback mechanism without resolving every causal step.

### Old fluorescence can outlast current activity

Reporter R is a stock of fluorescent material. Its amount reflects both earlier production and how much has since disappeared, so fluorescence is not a direct reading of A's current activity. [B19-S1]

If new production completely stops and the stated decay calibration applies, thirty minutes contain three ten-minute halvings:

\[
100\times\left(\frac12\right)^3=12.5\text{ units}.
\]

Thus the sequence is 100, 50, 25, and 12.5 units at zero, ten, twenty, and thirty minutes. A fractional amount is entirely valid for this measurement; the prediction need not be an integer.

The measured 13 units is 0.5 units above that prediction. It is close numerically, but the packet supplies no measurement precision or uncertainty with which to judge that difference. We cannot declare exact agreement, assign statistical significance, or claim the calibration is refuted merely because 12.5 is not an integer. [B19-S1; B19-S3]

Remaining fluorescence does not prove continued feedback: old reporter alone predicts substantial residual fluorescence even with no new production. Conversely, the decline does not prove that the inhibitor directly inhibited A. Its inhibition of reporter production was validated in a cell-free assay, not fully characterized inside the complete device; this batch has no direct A measurement. A could conceivably remain active while reporter production is blocked, or other effects could occur. Neither the inhibitor's internal mechanism nor complete cessation of reporter production in this batch is established. [B19-S3]

### What the next interventions would resolve

Removing B only after the persistent state is established would target **continued maintenance** more directly. If direct A activity then fell, compared with suitable controls for the removal procedure, that would strengthen the claim that ongoing B action is needed to maintain an already active switch. Measuring newly made R separately from old R, alongside A itself, would distinguish current production from a fading record of past activity. These experiments are proposed, not completed. [B19-S4]

The demonstrated persistence lasts two hours. Unlimited persistence, the necessary energy and material supply, and all possible alternative starting routes remain untested. A packaging award supplies no evidence for a “perpetual memory” claim. [B19-S2; B19-S4]
