**The analog total is exactly 180,000 J = 50 Wh = 0.050 kWh under the supplied calibration and hold model.** It is compatible with the register's conservative range, although it lies at that range's lower boundary.

### Calibration and energy over the requested interval

Applying \(P=2V-200\), with V in millivolts and P in watts, gives: [A14-CALIBRATION] [A14-SAMPLES]

Under the hold model, each power lasts until the next sample. Energy is power multiplied by elapsed time, since one watt is one joule per second. The 120-second reading starts a new level outside the interval [0,120), so it contributes no energy to this total. [A14-CALIBRATION] [A14-SAMPLES]

\]

The time-weighted mean power is

### Two distinct spreadsheet errors

The spreadsheet's unweighted mean is

\[
(1{,}000+2{,}000+1{,}000+0)/4=1{,}000\ \mathrm{W}.
\]

Its calculation then produces \(1{,}000\times120/1{,}000=120\), labeled kWh. But W × seconds gives joules; dividing by 1,000 converts joules to **kilojoules**, not kilowatt-hours. Conversion from joules to kWh requires division by **3,600,000**. [A14-SPREADSHEET] [A14-SAMPLES]

Fixing only that divisor gives \(120{,}000/3{,}600{,}000=0.033333\ldots\) kWh, still incorrect. The mean power remains wrong: the four sample values do not represent equal durations. Their correct time weights are **¼, ½, ¼, and 0**, respectively. The high-power interval lasts twice as long as either other interval, while the final reading lasts no time within the target interval. Even simply dropping the endpoint and averaging the remaining three values would not correct the unequal weighting.

These are errors in units and time weighting, not rounding discrepancies.

### What trapezoidal integration assumes

Trapezoidal integration averages the power at the two ends of each interval, producing

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{trap}}
&=\frac{1{,}000+2{,}000}{2}(30)
+\frac{2{,}000+1{,}000}{2}(60)
+\frac{1{,}000+0}{2}(30)\\
&=45{,}000+90{,}000+15{,}000\\
&=\boxed{150{,}000\ \mathrm{J}}\\
&=41.666\ldots\ \mathrm{Wh}
=0.041666\ldots\ \mathrm{kWh}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This assumes power changes linearly between samples. In particular, the 0 W endpoint influences the calculation by implying a gradual decline during the preceding 30 seconds. The endpoint itself still occupies no duration. Under the supplied hold model, power instead stays at 1,000 W throughout [90,120) and changes at 120 s. Rectangles therefore give the exact model-based integral here; trapezoids describe a different power history, not an automatic accuracy improvement. [A14-CALIBRATION] [A14-SAMPLES]

### Comparison with the register

The observed register increase is

\[
12.052-12.000=0.052\ \mathrm{kWh}.
\]

Each endpoint can differ from its true cumulative value by up to 0.001 kWh. In a difference, the two errors can act in opposite directions, giving a conservative possible error of

\[
0.001+0.001=0.002\ \mathrm{kWh}.
\]

The true register increase can therefore lie anywhere in

\[
\boxed{[0.050,\ 0.054]\ \mathrm{kWh}}.
\]

The analog total of 0.050 kWh is inside this range, exactly at its lower boundary. For example, true cumulative readings of 12.001 and 12.051 kWh would satisfy both display-error bounds and yield that increase. The packet therefore **does not establish an inconsistency between the instruments**. It also supplies no probability distribution from which to assign a confidence level or conclude that boundary errors are unlikely. [A14-REGISTER]

In a real installation, exact arithmetic would remain conditional on the measurement model. If calibration or the hold assumption were approximate, their errors would also need assessment: check the calibration, time alignment, and actual power changes between samples before attributing a discrepancy to an instrument. Agreement within the register bounds does not by itself validate those assumptions, just as a discrepancy would not by itself identify which instrument or assumption failed.
