The scene makes unanimity appear by **turning agreement about an urgent problem into a record of agreement with a particular proposal**. Yet what people understand, what they express, and what the minutes preserve never securely coincide.

## How agreement is produced

The YES cards precede any discussion: they are already on the chairs when the lights rise. Placing them on the table therefore displays assent without showing that anyone chose YES over an alternative. The closed cupboard labeled OTHER CARDS makes alternatives visible but unavailable. These arrangements support a reading of a procedure that steers participation toward one answer. They do not prove what each actor privately believes. [B27-S1]

The Clerk's opening claim concerns only the need to do “something.” The Carpenter begins to agree about the roof, but the bell interrupts his unfinished sentence. “Agreement recorded” removes the qualification before the audience can hear it. Later, agreeing that they cannot wait all winter establishes urgency, but the same formula again turns that limited statement into apparently sufficient agreement. Repetition gives the Clerk's words the force of an administrative routine: the record is being completed even while the substance remains unsettled. [B27-S1, B27-S2]

That distinction matters because the motion moves from roof repair to removing the east wall. The audience cannot hear three intervening lines, so it cannot assess how those actions are connected. The wet page and the Clerk's claim that only its dry part is affordable also bind restricted access to the proposal to material scarcity. The scene makes incomplete understanding a practical feature of the meeting; it does not let us fill in the missing text. [B27-S1, B27-S2]

## What the minutes leave out

“All voices present agreed” and “No objection was entered” narrow the relevant evidence to recognized voices and recorded objections. But a failure to enter an objection is not proof that nobody objected: questions are interrupted, alternatives remain inaccessible, and a blank card covers a YES. The child's gesture could withhold assent, refuse the available answer, or register uncertainty; the script does not give it one fixed meaning. No final vote is shown. [B27-S2, B27-S3, B27-S4]

The actors' changing chairs during the reading prevents seating from providing a stable picture of who constitutes the agreeing body. The Teacher's “Present when?” challenges the minutes' compression of participation across time. Meanwhile, the child's stillness offers a visible contrast to that shifting arrangement. The record supplies a settled collective subject—“all”—where the performance keeps its membership uncertain. [B27-S3]

## Shared necessity and unequal shelter

A strong reading is that the process excludes people and possible answers while claiming unanimity. But the performed text complicates assigning all responsibility to one corrupt official. The Clerk must not sneer, the rain is a real problem for everyone, and the Carpenter's handling of bowls shows that immediate practical work matters. The Treasurer has the key but cites water beneath the cupboard; the script leaves genuine danger and excuse unresolved. The discarded accusation of dishonesty is not an event or an established motive in this version, and the publisher's promotional description cannot settle those ambiguities. [B27-S1, B27-S2, B27-S4]

The final table-roof makes collective action tangible. The Clerk shelters with the others, sharing their vulnerability. Yet the child stands outside: the group's cooperation produces protection whose benefits are unevenly distributed. That exclusion is visible even though its intention is not specified. The sentence that must “hold water” links an account that sounds defensible with the urgent need for something that actually shelters people. [B27-S3]

Addressing the audience, the child asks how a collective “we” accommodates empty places. The chairs can suggest absent or unrepresented people without identifying particular groups. The blackout leaves both practical and political questions open: what the full motion would authorize, whether the cupboard could safely be opened, and whose participation and protection the declared agreement includes.
