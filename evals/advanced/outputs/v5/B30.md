**Between 100 and 500 learners must have both flags.** Overlap is therefore unavoidable, but the certified totals do not establish whether it exceeds 300. Neither a confident claim that the formal opening trigger is met nor a confident claim that it is unmet is justified.

## Deriving the possible overlap

Let \(x\) be the number with both flags. Adding 600 and 500 counts each learner with both flags twice, so the number with at least one flag is

\[
600+500-x=1{,}100-x.
\]

That number cannot exceed the population of 1,000, which requires \(x\geq100\). Conversely, the overlap cannot exceed either flag's total, so \(x\leq\min(600,500)=500\). Thus

\[
\boxed{100\leq x\leq500}.
\]

The sum of 1,100 is a count of flag memberships, not distinct learners. It does not contradict the certified population total. [B30-S1]

For any proposed overlap \(x\), the other groups must contain:

- A only: \(600-x\).
- B only: \(500-x\).
- Neither: \(x-100\).

All four groups are nonnegative for every integer \(x\) from 100 through 500. The bounds are therefore attainable, not merely loose limits.

## Feasible populations give different decisions

These are possible compositions, not observed cross-tabulations. Each row totals 1,000 learners and contains exactly 600 with A and 500 with B. [B30-S1]

| Possible composition | Both | A only | B only | Neither | More than 300 with both? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Smallest overlap | 100 | 500 | 400 | 0 | No |
| Exactly at the boundary | 300 | 300 | 200 | 200 | No |
| Just above the boundary | 301 | 299 | 199 | 201 | Yes |
| Largest overlap | 500 | 100 | 0 | 400 | Yes |

In particular, the 300-person and 301-person overlap cases satisfy precisely the same supplied totals but yield opposite trigger decisions. The rule requires **more than 300**, so exactly 300 is insufficient. The informal summary neither amends that requirement nor clearly identifies the relevant eligibility group. [B30-S2]

## Why the extra claims do not determine the answer

Multiplying \(0.6\times0.5\times1{,}000=300\) adds an independence assumption: having one flag would not change the probability of having the other. The supplied totals do not establish that relationship. A simulation that independently assigns flags builds this assumption into its artificial populations. Its average can estimate the model's expected overlap more precisely, but cannot discover the actual population's overlap. Even under a random independent-assignment model, an expected count is not a guarantee of the realized count. [B30-S3]

The feasible populations above expose the evidence gap: the observed margins alone cannot choose between them. Running more simulations under one assumed relationship does not provide the missing relationship.

The discussion group's 80 self-reported cases out of 100 cannot be projected to all learners or treated as random validation. Recruitment through an intensive-support channel, unverified flags, and absent links to administrative records prevent it from establishing the population overlap. Satisfaction measures provide no overlap information either. [B30-S4]

## The minimum additional information

For this decision, the administrator needs only a **certified yes-or-no answer to: “Do at least 301 currently enrolled learners have both flags?”** An exact overlap count would also settle the issue, but contains more information than the threshold decision requires. A certified lower bound of at least 301, or an upper bound of at most 300, would likewise be sufficient. [B30-S2]

An authorized data holder could evaluate that condition internally and release just the aggregate result. The administrator does not need learners' names, linked identifiers, additional personal attributes, or the complete cross-tabulation. Restrictions on sharing individual records leave useful mathematical bounds available; those bounds simply straddle this particular policy threshold.
