The service ended the month with **80 accounts and 8,400 units of contracted monthly recurring revenue**. Its opening customers generated more recurring revenue at month-end despite losing seven accounts: expansion by four Enterprise accounts outweighed the canceled accounts' revenue. Revenue retention above 100% therefore does not mean every customer stayed.

### Closing accounts and recurring revenue

First keep the opening cohort—the same 65 accounts present at the start—separate from newly acquired accounts. Stable account identifiers mean an expansion does not create a new customer. [B21-S1]

| Opening-cohort group | Surviving accounts | Closing recurring revenue, units per month |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Starter | 45 | 45 × 20 = 900 |
| Pro | 9 | 9 × 100 = 900 |
| Enterprise | 4 | 6,000 |
| **Opening-cohort total** | **58** | **7,800** |

These totals follow from [B21-S2]. The seven cancellations comprise five Starter accounts, one Pro account, and one Enterprise account. Their opening recurring revenue was:

\[
5(20)+1(100)+1(1{,}000)=1{,}200\text{ units per month}.
\]

The four surviving Enterprise accounts expanded from 4,000 to 6,000 units per month, adding 2,000. Thus the opening cohort's revenue reconciles as:

\[
7{,}000-1{,}200+2{,}000=7{,}800\text{ units per month}.
\]

The 22 new accounts add another \(20(20)+2(100)=600\) units per month. Across all accounts, the closing totals are therefore \(58+22=80\) accounts and \(7{,}800+600=8{,}400\) units per month. [B21-S1–S3]

### Three retention measures answer different questions

Using the supplied definitions, with percentages rounded to two decimal places:

| Metric | Calculation | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account retention** | 58 ÷ 65 = **89.23%** | How many opening accounts survived |
| **Net recurring-revenue retention** | 7,800 ÷ 7,000 = **111.43%** | How much opening-cohort recurring revenue remains after cancellations and expansion |
| **Gross recurring-revenue retention** | 5,800 ÷ 7,000 = **82.86%** | How much opening-cohort recurring revenue remains when expansion is excluded |

For gross retention, the surviving Starter and Pro accounts contribute 900 each, and the surviving Enterprise accounts count at their combined opening amount of 4,000: \(900+900+4{,}000=5{,}800\). No surviving account contracted, so the loss from 7,000 to 5,800 comes entirely from cancellations. [B21-S1; B21-S2; B21-S4]

Account retention gives each account one count. Revenue retention gives larger contracts more influence. Here, 2,000 units of expansion outweigh 1,200 units lost to cancellations, producing a net cohort increase of 800 units even though seven customers left. The numbers establish that coexistence; they do not establish why anyone stayed or left.

The slide's \(8{,}400/7{,}000=120\%\) includes new customers. An honest label is **“closing contracted monthly recurring revenue as a percentage of opening recurring revenue,”** or simply **“20% growth in contracted monthly recurring revenue.”** It combines expansion, cancellations, and acquisition. Calling it retention would credit newly acquired customers for retaining revenue from customers who were already there. [B21-S3; B21-S4]

### What the dashboard cannot establish

- **Cash collection:** These contract totals do not give the cash received this month. Annual prepayments and collections of prior overdue invoices can move cash independently of current monthly contract amounts. A receipts ledger, with payment dates and the periods covered, is needed. [B21-S3]
- **Earned revenue during the month:** The opening and closing snapshots omit when upgrades and cancellations took effect. They therefore cannot reconstruct revenue earned between those dates. Obtain dated contract changes and the applicable revenue-allocation records. [B21-S2]
- **Profit:** Neither recurring-revenue growth nor cash collection establishes higher profit. The packet lacks earned-revenue and cost totals; increased support workload alone does not quantify costs. Compare revenue and costs for the same reporting period. [B21-S2; B21-S3]
- **Customer satisfaction and cancellation reasons:** Praise describes the active accounts that responded. Canceled accounts were excluded, and the response rate is unknown, so the survey cannot establish satisfaction across the opening cohort or explain cancellation behavior. Seven accounts did cancel; no timing evidence establishes that cancellations subsequently stopped. Obtain invitation and response counts, feedback from canceled accounts, and dated cancellation reasons. [B21-S2; B21-S3]
- **Future durability:** This is one month's result, with the expansion concentrated in four Enterprise accounts. It does not show that their expansion will persist, that new accounts will remain, or that future cancellations will be offset. Track repeated cohorts, renewals, plan-level cancellations, and account-level expansion over time. [B21-S1–S3]

A supported headline is: **“Opening-cohort account retention was 89.23%; expansion lifted net recurring-revenue retention to 111.43%. Including new customers, contracted monthly recurring revenue grew 20%.”**
