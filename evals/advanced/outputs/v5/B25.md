**Put A through the scanner first, then review A before B: this meets both nominal deadlines.** Both scanner orders can finish the project at hour 16, but that does not mean they meet the same individual deadlines.

## Nominal schedules

The manager's 22 hours is the sum of all operation durations: 13 for A and 9 for B. It is not elapsed project time, because OCR can overlap scanning and review. Each calibration must remain immediately before its own scan. [B25-S1, B25-S2]

| Scanner order | Job | Calibration | Scanning | OCR | Review | Completion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, then B | A | 0–1 | 1–5 | 5–11 | 11–13 | 13 |
| A, then B | B | 5–6 | 6–8 | 8–11 | 13–16 | 16 |
| B, then A | B | 0–1 | 1–3 | 3–6 | 6–9 | 9 |
| B, then A | A | 3–4 | 4–8 | 8–14 | 14–16 | 16 |

All intervals are hours after the start. These schedules follow the stated resource and precedence rules. [B25-S1, B25-S2]

With **A first**, both OCR jobs finish at hour 11. The single reviewer cannot review them simultaneously. Reviewing A from 11 to 13 and B from 13 to 16 meets both deadlines exactly. If the reviewer instead takes B from 11 to 14 and A from 14 to 16, the project still finishes at 16, but A misses its deadline by three hours.

With **B first**, B finishes much earlier, at hour 9. However, A cannot start OCR until hour 8, so its earliest review is 14–16. A again misses its deadline. An early first completion and an early final completion are different objectives from meeting every job's deadline.

## What the proposed changes accomplish

**Another OCR computer does not improve these schedules.** Existing capacity already runs both OCR jobs simultaneously without slowing either one. More capacity changes neither their durations nor the scanner and reviewer constraints. [B25-S1, B25-S3]

**Expediting A creates a useful nominal improvement with A first.** Keep the scanner schedule above, run A's OCR from 5 to 9, and review A from 9 to 11. B's OCR still ends at 11, so review B from 11 to 14. A finishes at 11 and B at 14, two hours earlier each. The service therefore creates two hours of nominal deadline margin for both jobs. Its fee is 100, but the packet gives no monetary value for that improvement, so it does not establish that paying is profitable. [B25-S3]

## Nominal success is not a guarantee

Let \(d\) be A's actual ordinary OCR duration in hours. Even with A first and no reviewer delay, its earliest possible completion is

\[
1+4+d+2=7+d.
\]

Meeting hour 13 on this earliest path requires \(d\leq6\). The supplied range includes \(d=9\), which makes even this earliest completion hour 16. **The ordinary process cannot guarantee A's deadline across the stated range.** [B25-S1, B25-S4]

No lateness probability follows without a duration distribution. Excellent uptime does not establish a processing-time bound, and the expedited service's four-hour quote is also nominal, so it supplies no worst-case guarantee either. [B25-S4]
