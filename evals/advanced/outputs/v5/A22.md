**J1 executes two failed attempts and expires at time 6 before its third attempt starts.** Three attempts is a maximum, not a promise: its third attempt would finish at time 7, after its deadline of 6. J2 succeeds on its second attempt, and J3 succeeds on its first. [A22-RETRY; A22-DEADLINE]

## Actual worker timeline

Every executed attempt occupies the worker for one second. The queue chooses the smallest eligibility time first, using lexical job identifier and then attempt number only to break ties. [A22-SCHEDULER]

| Attempt or event | Scheduled eligibility | Actual start | Completion | Result and next action |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| J1, attempt 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | Failure; schedule attempt 2 for `1 + 1 = 2`. |
| J2, attempt 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | Failure; schedule attempt 2 for `2 + 1 = 3`. |
| J3, attempt 1 | 0 | 2 | 3 | Success; J3 ends. |
| J1, attempt 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Failure; schedule attempt 3 for `4 + 2 = 6`. |
| J2, attempt 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Success; J2 ends. |
| Worker idle | Next work eligible at 6 | — | — | No eligible attempt during `[5, 6)`. |
| J1, attempt 3 selected at 6 | 6 | **Does not start** | **None** | Its proposed completion is `6 + 1 = 7 > 6`; expire J1 immediately. |

Two queue decisions explain the apparent surprises:

- At time 2, J3's initial attempt has eligibility time **0**, while J1's retry has eligibility time **2**. J3 runs first because its scheduled time is earlier.
- At time 3, J1's retry has eligibility time **2**, ahead of J2's retry at **3**, so J1 runs next.

An eligibility time permits work to begin from that time onward; it does not reserve a start time. Deadlines also do not reorder the queue. [A22-SCHEDULER; A22-DEADLINE]

At time 4, J1's next attempt is enqueued for time 6 even though it will not fit before the deadline. The supplied rule requires checking only when it is selected, so expiration occurs at **6**, not at enqueue time 4. Expiration executes no attempt and consumes no one-second execution interval. All work is resolved by time 6 after five seconds of execution and one second of idle time.

## Which listed outcomes actually occur?

| Job | Executed outcomes | Unexecuted outcomes | Final state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | Failure, failure | The listed third-attempt success remains hypothetical. | Expired after two attempts. |
| J2 | Failure, success | None of its listed outcomes remain unused. | Succeeded after two attempts. |
| J3 | Success | None of its listed outcomes remain unused. | Succeeded after one attempt. |

An outcome in the input describes what would happen **if** that attempt executes. It cannot establish that scheduling and deadline checks will allow the attempt to start. [A22-INPUT]

## Evaluating the incident claims

**“Backoff schedules J1 at absolute times 1 and 2.” — Incorrect.** The numbers 1 and 2 are successive waiting durations, measured from each failed attempt's completion. J1's first failure completes at 1, producing eligibility at 2. Its second failure actually completes at 4, producing eligibility at 6. Queue waiting helps determine when that second failure occurs. [A22-RETRY]

**“J1's retry must run before J3.” — Incorrect.** Retrying gives no special priority. J3's eligibility time 0 precedes J1's retry eligibility time 2. [A22-SCHEDULER]

**“Maximum attempts guarantees three tries and eventual success.” — Incorrect.** The limit caps executed attempts, including the initial attempt. Success can end a job earlier; a deadline can prevent a permitted attempt from executing. The absolute deadline sets the latest allowed completion time. Completion exactly at time 6 would be allowed for J1, but starting its third attempt at time 6 would complete at 7. [A22-RETRY; A22-DEADLINE]

Removing the deadline check would let J1's third attempt run from 6 to 7 and realize its listed success. That would change the contract and permit a late result. A successful graph is not sufficient justification; the team must decide whether a late result still has value and whether the deadline may legitimately be extended.

## Production handling and instrumentation

A sensible expiration policy records a terminal **expired** state distinct from execution failure, including the deadline, attempts actually started, pending attempt number, and proposed completion that exceeded the deadline. Depending on the job's purpose, retain it for review, notify the requester, or route it to an expired-job queue. Resubmission with a new deadline should be an explicit policy decision; if earlier attempts may have produced external effects, the resubmission also needs duplicate-effect protection.

Record timestamps for initial enqueue, retry scheduling, scheduled eligibility, selection, actual start, completion, and expiration, alongside job and attempt identifiers and outcomes. Derive separate durations:

- **Retry delay:** failed completion to the next scheduled eligibility time.
- **Eligible queue waiting:** eligibility to selection/start while the attempt is ready but has not received the worker.
- **Execution time:** actual start to completion, only for an executed attempt.

For J1's first retry, completion at 1 to eligibility at 2 is **one second of backoff**; eligibility at 2 to start at 3 is **one second of queue waiting**; start at 3 to completion at 4 is **one second of execution**. Counting all three as “retry delay” would hide the reason for the delay. Track retries scheduled separately from attempts started and jobs expired so an enqueued third attempt cannot appear as an executed third attempt.

In a system with many workers or clients, random variation in retry delays—jitter—can spread retries that would otherwise hit a recovering shared dependency simultaneously after correlated failures. This exercise specifies no jitter, so it does not change any time in the deterministic timeline above.
