**The window emits 12, then a replacement total of 15. It is deleted at step 7; the amount 4 at step 8 is dropped.** Neither emission nor deletion proves that no more relevant events can exist. They reflect the protocol’s chosen rules.

### Step-by-step trace

The watermark is the protocol’s progress marker. Each active partition proposes its greatest observed timestamp minus 10; the global watermark takes the minimum of those proposals without ever moving backward. Data is judged using the watermark **before** that data updates partition state. [A08-RULES]

Initially, both partition maxima are 0, both are active, and the global watermark is −10.

| Step | p0 maximum afterward | p1 maximum and status afterward | Global watermark afterward | Fate of [0,60) |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1: p0 data, time 20, amount 5 | 20 | 0, active | −10 | Accept; stored total 5. No output. |
| 2: p1 data, time 50, amount 7 | 20 | 50, active | 10 | Accept; stored total 12. No output. |
| 3: p0 heartbeat 80 | 80 | 50, active | 40 | Keep total 12. No output. |
| 4: p1 heartbeat 75 | 80 | 75, active | 65 | First crossing of 60: emit initial total **12** and retain state. |
| 5: p0 data, time 40, amount 3 | 80 | 75, active | 65 | Accept; emit replacement total **15**. |
| 6: p1 idle | 80 | 75, idle | 70 | Retain total 15. No output. |
| 7: p0 heartbeat 100 | 100 | 75, idle | 90 | Delete window state permanently. No output. |
| 8: p1 data, time 55, amount 4 | 100 | 75, active | 90 | Drop the record; increment the dropped-record count by one. No output. |

The table follows the message order and boundary rules in [A08-INPUT; A08-RULES].

For example, after step 3 the proposals are 70 from p0 and 40 from p1, so the watermark is 40. After step 4 they are 70 and 65, so it advances to 65 and triggers the first emission.

### Why a correction is still possible

At step 5, event time 40 belongs to [0,60), and the current watermark is 65. The window has emitted, but its state remains available while the watermark is below 80. The amount 3 therefore changes the total from 12 to 15 and immediately produces a replacement. Its timestamp does not lower p0’s maximum of 80. Being older than the watermark is not, by itself, this protocol’s rejection rule. [A08-RULES]

Marking p1 idle at step 6 removes its proposal of 65 from the minimum. That allows p0’s proposal of 70 to advance the watermark, followed by 90 at step 7. Without that exclusion, p1’s unchanged proposal would keep the watermark at 65. This is how a silent partition stops holding back progress. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

At step 8, the record is first judged against the existing watermark of 90, after the window has already been deleted. Reactivation cannot undo that decision. Nor does timestamp 55 reduce p1’s previous maximum of 75. The active proposals become 90 and 65, but monotonicity keeps the global watermark at 90. The window is not recreated, and the amount 4 never enters an emitted total. [A08-RULES]

### Downstream must replace totals safely

Adding the two emitted totals gives **12 + 15 = 27**, although the accepted amounts sum to **5 + 7 + 3 = 15**. Transport redelivery can inflate the sum further. The messages contain complete window totals, so addition is incorrect. [A08-DOWNSTREAM; A08-INPUT]

A suitable output contract would identify both the window and a monotonically increasing revision, for example:

| Window | Revision | Replacement total |
|---|---:|---:|
| [0,60) | 1 | 12 |
| [0,60) | 2 | 15 |

Retries must preserve the same window, revision, and payload. The producer must preserve revision identity across recovery. The consumer should atomically compare each incoming revision with the greatest revision stored for that window:

- A newer revision replaces the stored total.
- An identical message at the same revision is a no-op.
- An older revision is ignored, so delayed delivery cannot overwrite a correction.
- The same revision with different contents is a conflict to investigate.

The current window-identifier-and-total format lacks enough information to distinguish a delayed old total from a newer correction in general. Adding durable revision semantics makes both corrections and redelivery safe. A total across windows should sum each window’s latest stored total.

### What the late-data policy trades away

The policy allows bounded storage and progress despite an idle partition, at the cost of excluding relevant records that return after deletion. Step 8 demonstrates that cost: a valid event time is insufficient for acceptance once the retention boundary has passed.

The first emission is preliminary because corrections remain allowed. Deletion makes the result final **under this acceptance policy**, but is not evidence that the physical world cannot produce another event for the minute. Wall-clock time has no role in this exercise, and the rules emit no separate finalization message when state is deleted. [A08-RULES; A08-DOWNSTREAM]
