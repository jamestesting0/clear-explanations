**Restoring one necessary condition can change the pond community without restoring colony establishment.** In the proposed model, colonies need both adequate light and enough mineral M. A grazer intervention can restore the route to light while leaving M too low; an M intervention can leave light too low. That makes an unsuccessful single rescue compatible with the targeted mechanism mattering. The model remains a hypothesis to assess against these invented tank results. [B15-S1]

## What the interventions changed

There were four tanks per condition. The table reports condition means, not results for every tank. [B15-S2]

| Condition | Grazers | Film | Light | M | Established colonies |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Untreated | 10 | 1 | 80 | 8 | 40 |
| Warmed | 3 | 7 | 20 | 2 | 5 |
| Warmed + grazers | 10 | 1 | 80 | 2 | 6 |
| Warmed + M | 3 | 7 | 20 | 8 | 7 |
| Warmed + both | 10 | 1 | 80 | 8 | 38 |

Adding grazers restored grazer abundance, reduced film, and restored measured light to the untreated means. Its small colony response therefore does **not** show that grazers did nothing: substantial intermediate changes occurred. Under the two-requirement explanation, low M still prevents most colonies from establishing. Adding M alone produces the complementary situation: M is restored while film remains high and light low. [B15-S1; B15-S2]

With both additions, measured light and M are restored together and the colony mean rises to 38, close to the untreated mean of 40. This is consistent with two jointly limiting conditions. It does not prove exact restoration, or that the proposed network is the only explanation. [B15-S2; B15-S3]

## Why the combined gain need not equal the separate gains

Relative to warmed tanks, the separate colony gains are `6 − 5 = 1` and `7 − 5 = 2`. Predicting `5 + 1 + 2 = 8` assumes that each intervention has the same effect regardless of the other intervention. The proposed model gives a reason that assumption could fail. [B15-S1; B15-S2]

If adequate light helps colonies establish mainly when M is also adequate, restoring light has little final effect while M remains scarce. Once M is restored, the same improvement in light can make a large difference. This dependence of one intervention's effect on another is an **interaction**.

In the reported means, adding grazers changes colonies by 1 without added M, but by `38 − 7 = 31` with added M. The combined result exceeds the additive prediction by 30 colonies. Those are descriptive calculations; without individual tank results or uncertainty estimates, their sampling precision is unknown. The observed 38 is not mathematically impossible. [B15-S2]

## Which parts of the mechanism are supported?

- **Warming and the additions were directly tested as interventions.** Random assignment supports comparisons of their effects in these tanks. The grazer-addition comparisons support an effect on film and light, as well as a colony response that differs with M treatment. But they do not isolate every intermediate causal link. [B15-S2]
- **Frond shelter was bypassed.** Adding animals directly does not test whether warming reduced fronds or whether restoring fronds would restore grazers. The separate shelter pilot supports a grazer response to that pilot intervention, but shelter also shaded the tanks and colony establishment was not measured. It cannot establish the whole shelter-to-colony pathway. [B15-S1; B15-S3]
- **Film and light moved together, but light was not independently manipulated.** The results support the proposed pathway without proving that light alone explains the colony response to grazers. The film-eating video establishes that one grazer can perform the behavior; it does not measure its importance across tanks. [B15-S2; B15-S3]
- **The M treatment was tested, but its mechanism is not fully isolated.** It raised measured M and contributed to the strong combined response; unmeasured effects of adding M could also matter. Thus the dual rescue does not prove either a pure mineral mechanism or that frond loss is warming's only damaging effect. [B15-S1–S3]

## Two focused follow-up comparisons

1. **Vary light directly, with and without added M.** In warmed tanks, compare low and restored light at each M-treatment level, while keeping grazer and film conditions comparable and using matched handling controls. Measure actual light, M, and colony establishment. A light benefit chiefly when M is restored would test the joint-requirement explanation without relying on grazers to change light. This would still require checking accompanying effects of the M treatment before attributing everything specifically to mineral concentration.
2. **Add shelter while controlling its shading effect.** Compare warmed tanks with usable artificial shelter against a control that matches its shading but does not provide shelter. Keep M comparable and measure grazers, film, actual light, and subsequent colonies. This tests whether shelter itself increases grazers and whether that change carries through the proposed pathway; it addresses the link bypassed by direct grazer addition.

Both comparisons should report individual tank outcomes and uncertainty, so apparent differences can be assessed against tank-to-tank variation. The fundraising language provides no evidence about these mechanisms. [B15-S3; B15-S4]
