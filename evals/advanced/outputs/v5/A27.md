The degradation probabilities after the three readings are approximately **0.5625, 0.09031, and 0.5457**. Under the stated current-minute costs, the operator should **continue operation after minute 2 and inspect after minute 3**. The minute-4 prediction, assuming no intervention, is **0.4592**.

## Predict the state, then incorporate the reading

Let \(p_t\) be the probability of degradation at minute \(t\), after using all readings available through that minute. Let \(q_t\) be the probability just before that minute's reading, after allowing the state to change. Initially, \(p_0=0.10\).

The transition rule gives:

\[
q_t=0.80p_{t-1}+0.05(1-p_{t-1})
    =0.05+0.75p_{t-1}.
\]

The first term covers a previously degraded machine remaining degraded; the second covers a previously normal machine becoming degraded. This transition happens before **every** reading, including the first. [A27-STATE]

The sensor then changes the probability according to the observed reading:

\[
p_t=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{0.90q_t}{0.90q_t+0.10(1-q_t)},
&\text{alarm},\\[8pt]
\displaystyle\frac{0.10q_t}{0.10q_t+0.90(1-q_t)},
&\text{quiet}.
\end{cases}
\]

Each numerator is the probability of being degraded and producing that reading. Each denominator adds the two possible explanations of the reading: a degraded machine or a normal one. [A27-SENSOR]

Applying these steps, retaining precision between minutes:

| Minute | Reading | Predicted degradation, before reading | Updated degradation, after reading |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | Alarm | 0.1250 | 0.5625 |
| 2 | Quiet | 0.4719 | 0.09031 |
| 3 | Alarm | 0.1177 | 0.5457 |

For example, minute 2 starts with

\[
q_2=0.05+0.75(0.5625)=0.471875.
\]

The quiet reading then gives

\[
p_2=
\frac{0.10(0.471875)}
     {0.10(0.471875)+0.90(0.528125)}
=0.0903110048\ldots.
\]

That updated value feeds the next transition. It is neither discarded nor reused unchanged as the minute-3 prior.

## Where the engineers change the model

The first engineer's calculation is valid for a different assumption: **one fixed hidden state throughout all three tests**. Under that assumption, the alarm–quiet–alarm likelihood is \(0.9\times0.1\times0.9=0.081\) for degradation and \(0.1\times0.9\times0.1=0.009\) for normality. With a 0.10 prior, the calculation indeed gives:

\[
\frac{0.10(0.081)}{0.10(0.081)+0.90(0.009)}=0.50.
\]

But the supplied machine may change state before each reading. A quiet reading concerns the state at minute 2; it cannot simply cancel an alarm concerning another minute's state. Conditional independence given the **whole state sequence** permits multiplying the observation probabilities along a particular state history. It does not justify pretending that all histories contain one unchanging state, or that the readings are independent without conditioning on states. [A27-STATE, A27-SENSOR]

The second engineer discards earlier information by resetting the prior to 0.10 before each reading and also omits the required transition-based prediction. If 0.10 is used directly as the reading's prior, every alarm produces 0.50 and every quiet reading about 0.01220, regardless of the preceding observations. Those are not the probabilities from the stated sequential model.

## Immediate inspection decision

After a reading, let \(p\) denote the probability of **current** degradation. Inspection costs 3 units and prevents the current loss. Continuing has expected cost \(20p\): a 20-unit loss weighted by its probability. Therefore:

\[
\text{inspect when }3<20p,
\qquad\text{equivalently }p>0.15.
\]

At exactly 0.15, both choices have the same expected cost under this model. [A27-DECISION]

| Decision point | Current degradation probability | Expected cost of continuing | Inspection cost | Choice |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| After minute 2 | 0.090311… | 1.8062 units | 3 units | Continue |
| After minute 3 | 0.545660… | 10.9132 units | 3 units | Inspect |

A probability alone does not specify an action; the supplied costs make the comparison possible. These choices minimize expected current-minute cost under the stated assumptions. They are evaluated using the supplied history with no maintenance; actually intervening earlier would change the state information used in later predictions.

## Information available now versus later

**Filtering** estimates the current state using observations available so far. The minute-2 action therefore uses \(P(D_2\mid\text{alarm}_1,\text{quiet}_2)=0.09031\). The minute-3 alarm was not available then. [A27-OBSERVATIONS]

Using that later alarm to reassess minute 2 is **retrospective inference**, also called smoothing: estimating an earlier state with later evidence. It can be useful for an after-the-fact investigation, but it must not be described as the information the operator had at minute 2.

Finally, if no intervention occurs after minute 3:

\[
q_4=0.05+0.75p_3
   =0.05+0.75(0.5456603425\ldots)
   =0.4592452569\ldots\approx0.4592.
\]

This is a forecast for minute 4 before its reading, allowing another state transition. The immediate minute-3 decision concerns degradation **at minute 3**, so it uses \(p_3\approx0.5457\). Substituting the minute-4 forecast would price the risk at the wrong time horizon; moreover, the forecast's no-intervention condition would no longer hold if the recommended inspection were performed.
