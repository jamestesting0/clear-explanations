Under the enacted rules supplied here, **Alder requires an inspection certificate; Birch can use a transition declaration; Cedar's position depends first on where its workshop lies and, if it is in Port Meridian, on what the warehouse delivery actually represented.** A document's later publication date does not by itself change the governing rule.

### Which documents control?

The enacted ordinance establishes eligibility. The implementing office can specify forms, so its circular governs the declaration's required evidence and filing process, but it cannot move the ordinance's eligibility dates. The informal training slide is an incomplete summary: “orders received” cannot replace a signed purchase agreement, and its omission of physical delivery does not remove that condition. [B03-S1; B03-S2]

The May 28 amendment is explicitly a proposal requiring a separate council vote. The packet records no enactment or promulgation. The June 3 digest links only to that proposal, so its confident phrase “June extension” supplies no basis for applying a June 15 cutoff. The modernization brochure has no bearing on eligibility. [B03-S3]

The relevant dates perform different jobs:

| Date or event | What it determines |
| --- | --- |
| April 2 publication | The ordinance's publication date and the dividing date for eligible signed agreements: the agreement must be earlier than April 2 |
| June 1 commencement | The certificate requirement begins for commercial equipment first placed into service in the port district from that date |
| Physical customer delivery before June 1 | A separate condition for the transition alternative; warehouse arrival, dispatch, customs clearance, and invoicing do not substitute for it |
| First operation | When the equipment enters service; this is distinct from delivery and filing |
| July 15 filing limit | The last date for filing a qualifying declaration; it does not extend the agreement or delivery conditions |

The circular expressly allows filing after first use when the substantive transition conditions were met. [B03-S1; B03-S2]

### Alder: certificate required

Alder's March 29 signed agreement satisfies the agreement condition. Its physical delivery to the Port Meridian customer on June 3 does **not** satisfy delivery before June 1. Arrival at the port warehouse on May 29 cannot replace the stated customer-delivery date. Because Alder first operated commercially in Port Meridian on June 5, it falls under the certificate requirement and cannot use the documented transition alternative. [B03-S1; B03-S4]

The draft does not change that answer. Furthermore, its proposed extension concerns dock-repair delays, and the packet does not establish that Alder experienced such a delay. The packet also does not say whether Alder already has an inspection certificate: it establishes the requirement, not an actual certificate violation. [B03-S3; B03-S4]

### Birch: eligible for a transition declaration

Birch meets both substantive conditions: its signed agreement is dated April 1, before April 2, and its customer received it physically on May 31, before June 1. Its June 2 first use does not remove that eligibility. [B03-S1; B03-S4]

The July 10 appointment is within the July 15 filing period, and filing after first use is expressly permitted. Birch can therefore use the transition route, provided a complete declaration is actually filed on time with the signed agreement, proof of physical customer delivery, and serial number. An appointment is not proof that filing has occurred or that the documents are complete. [B03-S2; B03-S4]

### Cedar: two facts need checking

**First, establish the workshop's district.** Cedar first operated commercially on June 4, but the workshop's district assignment and the boundary map are missing. A warehouse postal address reading “Meridian Bay” does not establish where the workshop is situated within the legal district boundaries. [B03-S4]

- If the workshop is outside Port Meridian, this ordinance imposes no certificate requirement on that first use. Any rules for the district concerned must be checked separately; the packet does not provide Estuary's regime.
- If the workshop is inside Port Meridian, the June 4 first use brings Cedar within this ordinance, subject to the transition alternative. [B03-S1]

**Second, if Port Meridian's rules apply, establish physical customer delivery.** The March 20 agreement qualifies. But “delivered May 25 to a warehouse” does not say whether that was delivery to the customer or merely intermediate storage. [B03-S4]

If the customer physically received Cedar there on May 25—or otherwise received it before June 1—it can use a transition declaration, with the required evidence and timely filing. If physical customer delivery occurred on or after June 1, it needs an inspection certificate. Until the recipient and delivery date are established, the transition decision remains open. [B03-S1; B03-S2]

The team's decision process should therefore start with use and district, identify the governing enacted rule, then check the agreement and physical customer-delivery facts separately. Only after eligibility is established should the team check forms and filing. Publication dates help identify documents; they do not give an informal summary or an unenacted draft authority to change the ordinance.
