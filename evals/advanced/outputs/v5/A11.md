The model predicts **lower median render times with acceleration throughout 100–200 MiB, but lower arithmetic mean times only near the upper end of that range**. It describes differences between observed modes; it does not establish what switching the feature on would cause. [A11-MODEL] [A11-STUDY]

### Median durations and the interaction

Let μ be the fitted mean of ln(T), where T is measured in seconds. Substituting the mode indicator gives:

- Standard mode: μ = 4.00 + 0.20x.
- Accelerated mode: μ = 3.90 + 0.05x.

Here x = 0 at 100 MiB and x = 1 at 200 MiB. The conditional median duration is exp(μ), so the accelerated-to-standard median ratio is

\[
\exp\bigl((3.90+0.05x)-(4.00+0.20x)\bigr)
=\exp(-0.10-0.15x).
\]

Using the supplied model and identity gives the following rounded values. [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY]

| File size | Standard median | Accelerated median | Accelerated ÷ standard | Change in median |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB | 54.60 s | 49.40 s | 0.9048 | 9.52% lower |
| 200 MiB | 66.69 s | 51.94 s | 0.7788 | 22.12% lower |

The −0.10 coefficient is a difference in **log duration at 100 MiB**, not a saving of 0.10 seconds for every file. At 100 MiB, the predicted median saving is about 5.20 seconds; at 200 MiB it is about 14.75 seconds.

The size slope also uses the scaled variable x. Across the increase from 100 to 200 MiB:

- Standard mode’s median is multiplied by exp(0.20) ≈ 1.2214: a **22.14% increase**.
- Accelerated mode’s median is multiplied by exp(0.05) ≈ 1.0513: a **5.13% increase**.

The interaction −0.15 reduces the log-duration slope from 0.20 to 0.05 per additional **100 MiB**. Equivalently, that size increase multiplies the accelerated-to-standard median ratio by exp(−0.15) ≈ 0.8607, a 13.93% reduction in the ratio. It is not a further 15% saving per MiB. [A11-MODEL]

### Arithmetic means and batch totals

Exponentiating μ gives the median. For the arithmetic mean, the supplied lognormal identity requires adding half the residual variance before exponentiating. Those adjustments are 0.40²/2 = 0.08 for standard mode and 0.80²/2 = 0.32 for accelerated mode. [A11-IDENTITY] [A11-MODEL]

Thus the ratio of conditional arithmetic means is

\[
\frac{\exp(3.90+0.05x+0.32)}{\exp(4.00+0.20x+0.08)}
=\exp(0.14-0.15x).
\]

| File size | Standard arithmetic mean | Accelerated arithmetic mean | Accelerated ÷ standard |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB | 59.15 s | 68.03 s | 1.1503: 15.03% higher |
| 200 MiB | 72.24 s | 71.52 s | 0.9900: 1.00% lower |

Acceleration’s larger spread in log durations produces a heavier upper tail of long durations, which raises its arithmetic mean relative to its median. Consequently, its typical request can be faster while its mean is slower. The fitted means cross at x = 0.14/0.15, corresponding to approximately **193.3 MiB**.

Because each mode’s residual variance is constant across size, its arithmetic mean has the same proportional size increase as its median. To predict a batch’s total render duration, add the conditional arithmetic means for its requests; an overall mode comparison must also account for the batch’s file-size mix. The variance adjustment describes the distribution’s mean, not an extra coefficient for an individual observation. [A11-IDENTITY]

### What the evidence can establish

Users selected their mode. Graphics-card quality, model complexity, and retry history could influence both that choice and duration, yet were unrecorded. Controlling for file size alone therefore does not isolate a causal effect of acceleration. The fitted relationship also has no stated support outside **100–200 MiB**. [A11-STUDY] [A11-MODEL]

A more direct study would randomly assign acceleration on or off for comparable workloads, balance file sizes and hardware, and measure both medians and arithmetic means. If the same machine and file are tested in both modes, randomize run order and control warm-up effects. That would address the consequence of switching modes while reducing competing explanations.
