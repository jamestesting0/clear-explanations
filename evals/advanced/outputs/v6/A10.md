**The new workflow has a 10-percentage-point higher observed completion rate within each complexity group, yet a 30-point lower overall rate because it handled a much harder case mix.** Giving both workflows the same complexity weights removes that particular imbalance, but does not establish a causal benefit: urgency and staffing still differ within the groups. [A10-DATA; A10-ASSIGNMENT]

## What the observed rates say

The outcome throughout is completion within seven calendar days, and each denominator is the number of cases in the relevant workflow and complexity group. Calculating from [A10-DATA]:

| Complexity | Old workflow | New workflow | New minus old |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | `144/180 = 80%` | `18/20 = 90%` | **+10 percentage points** |
| Hard | `6/20 = 30%` | `72/180 = 40%` | **+10 percentage points** |

Within each row, the contrast compares that complexity group under the two observed assignments. It does not average easy and hard cases together.

The raw workflow rates use each workflow's **own observed case mix**:

| Summary | Easy/hard weights | Calculation | Completion rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old workflow, actual cases | 90% / 10% | `(144 + 6)/200 = 0.90(80%) + 0.10(30%)` | **75%** |
| New workflow, actual cases | 10% / 90% | `(18 + 72)/200 = 0.10(90%) + 0.90(40%)` | **45%** |

Thus the raw difference is `45% − 75% = −30 percentage points`. Both raw rates correctly describe their respective observed cohorts. [A10-DATA]

The reversal occurs because the new workflow's overall rate gives nine-tenths of its weight to hard cases, whose completion rates are lower under both workflows. The old workflow gives nine-tenths of its weight to easy cases. The within-group advantages and the overall disadvantage therefore answer different descriptive questions. This kind of reversal is often called **Simpson's paradox**; here the weighting arithmetic makes it explicit.

## Comparable summaries under the requested case mixes

Standardizing means taking each workflow's observed group rates and averaging them with the **same chosen case-mix weights**. If `w` is the proportion of easy cases, the calculation is:

\[
\text{standardized rate}
=w(\text{easy-case rate})+(1-w)(\text{hard-case rate}).
\]

| Target population | Easy/hard weights for both workflows | Old workflow | New workflow | New minus old |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Half easy, half hard | 50% / 50% | `0.50(80%) + 0.50(30%) = 55%` | `0.50(90%) + 0.50(40%) = 65%` | **+10 points** |
| Expected next-quarter mix | 20% / 80% | `0.20(80%) + 0.80(30%) = 40%` | `0.20(90%) + 0.80(40%) = 50%` | **+10 points** |

These are derived from [A10-DATA] using the targets in [A10-PLANNING]. The first target also happens to match the pooled complexity distribution in this table: 200 easy and 200 hard cases. It gives a comparison with complexity held to equal proportions. The second describes the requested next-quarter mix under the convention that the observed group rates are the rates being summarized.

The difference is 10 points for both targets because each group's observed difference is exactly 10 points. The absolute rates differ because the second target contains more hard cases. Neither calculation establishes that a rollout will preserve those group rates; [A10-PLANNING] explicitly makes that a calculation convention rather than an intervention claim.

## Assessing the three claims

**The director's arithmetic is right; the causal conclusion is unsupported.** The new cohort's raw rate is 30 points lower. But the cohorts had different complexity distributions because routing was deliberate, so that difference does not show that switching a case to the new workflow harms it. The raw rate remains useful for describing what happened to each team's actual cases. [A10-DATA; A10-ASSIGNMENT]

**The implementation lead overstates both causality and individual benefit.** The higher rates in both groups are observed associations. Within each complexity group, urgency influenced routing to a specialist working exclusively with the new workflow, and urgency can affect completion. Staff differences may matter too. Adjusting only for complexity therefore leaves plausible alternative explanations for the observed advantage; the true workflow effect could differ in size or even direction. Complexity being measured before assignment makes it an eligible baseline characteristic, but does not prove it captures every source of selection. [A10-ASSIGNMENT]

Calling the adjusted 10-point difference the proven effect of switching a particular case would add a second error. Even a well-established average causal benefit would not show that every case benefits. For each case, the relevant causal comparison is its completion outcome under the new workflow versus its outcome under the old; only one of those outcomes is observed. A group average can combine cases helped, cases harmed, and cases unaffected. These are service cases, and the stray patient wording supplies no additional evidence. [A10-CLAIMS]

**The analyst needs to specify a population and preserve the comparison.** Averaging all four percentages gives `(80 + 30 + 90 + 40)/4 = 60%`. That gives equal weight to the four workflow-by-complexity cells. It could summarize a hypothetical population equally distributed across those cells, but produces one overall number rather than a comparison between workflows. It happens also to equal the pooled observed rate, `240/400 = 60%`, in this table; that coincidence does not make equal cell weights generally appropriate. [A10-DATA]

If the analyst instead means averaging the two complexity rates **separately for each workflow**, that correctly produces 55% and 65% for the 50/50 target. It does not produce the next-quarter comparison, which requires 20/80 weights. There is no single target-free “adjusted rate.” [A10-PLANNING]

## Evidence that would support a rollout decision

The most direct next step is a prospective randomized pilot with the intervention and staffing policy clearly defined. Assign cases randomly to workflows within each complexity group, prevent discretionary rerouting from undoing that assignment, and retain complete seven-day follow-up. Random assignment makes treatment choice independent of pre-existing urgency and other case characteristics in expectation; it supports an average causal comparison while leaving finite-sample uncertainty to be measured.

However, three proposed improvements answer different questions:

| Design or evidence | What it can clarify | Remaining distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random assignment within complexity groups | Average effect of the assigned intervention as actually delivered | If the specialist remains exclusive to the new arm, this estimates the workflow-and-staffing package |
| Measure relevant pre-assignment variables | Compare cases more closely on urgency, initial condition, workload, staff availability, and routing conditions | Observational adjustment still relies on having measured the relevant common causes and having comparable cases under both workflows |
| Separate specialist access from workflow | Workflow effects under comparable staffing, and the specialist's contribution | Requires a design in which workflow and specialist access are not inseparable |

For the third question, one practical design is to have multiple staff, including the specialist where feasible, handle randomly assigned cases under both workflows. Another is to randomize workflow and specialist access separately, allowing the study to assess whether a workflow's benefit depends on who delivers it. Simply recording specialist identity in the existing data cannot create the missing comparison if that specialist never uses the old workflow. [A10-ASSIGNMENT]

The rollout decision should specify whether it concerns the **whole package with specialist capacity** or a **workflow change using ordinary staffing**. A randomized package comparison may be exactly the right study for the first decision and insufficient for the second. The planned workload and 20/80 mix should also inform the pilot and its reporting, since rates observed with extra capacity need not persist at rollout scale. A larger table generated by the same routing would improve precision without resolving those routing and staffing explanations. [A10-ASSIGNMENT; A10-PLANNING]
