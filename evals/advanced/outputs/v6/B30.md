**Between 100 and 500 learners must have both flags.** Some overlap is certain, but the certified totals do not determine whether it exceeds 300. A confident decision that the formal opening condition is met—or that it is not met—is therefore unsupported by this packet. [B30-S1, B30-S2]

## Deriving the possible overlap

Let \(x\) be the number with both flags. The four groups must then contain:

| Group | Number of learners |
| --- | ---: |
| Both A and B | \(x\) |
| A only | \(600-x\) |
| B only | \(500-x\) |
| Neither | \(x-100\) |

The last row follows because the number with at least one flag is \(600+500-x=1,100-x\); subtracting that from the population of 1,000 leaves \(x-100\). [B30-S1; calculation]

Every group must have a nonnegative count. “Neither” requires \(x\geq100\), while “B only” requires \(x\leq500\). The A count also requires \(x\leq600\), which is less restrictive. Therefore,

\[
\boxed{100\leq x\leq500.}
\]

Adding 600 and 500 gives 1,100 **flag memberships**, not 1,100 distinct learners. Anyone with both flags appears twice in that sum. At least 100 such double counts are necessary for those memberships to fit into 1,000 people; this does not make the certified totals inconsistent. [B30-S1]

## Two complete populations with different decisions

Both endpoints are feasible:

| Group | Population with minimum overlap | Population with maximum overlap |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Both flags | 100 | 500 |
| A only | 500 | 100 |
| B only | 400 | 0 |
| Neither | 0 | 400 |
| **Total population** | **1,000** | **1,000** |
| Total with A | 600 | 600 |
| Total with B | 500 | 500 |
| **More than 300 with both?** | **No** | **Yes** |

These constructions satisfy all certified totals while giving opposite answers to the formal trigger. Unavoidable overlap therefore does not imply enough overlap to open the center. [B30-S1, B30-S2; constructions]

The boundary is strict: **exactly 300 does not qualify**. It is also feasible—300 with both, 300 with A only, 200 with B only, and 200 with neither. The later informal summary neither amends the rule nor clearly preserves its definition of eligibility, so it does not replace “more than 300 with both flags.” [B30-S2]

## Why the proposed estimates do not settle it

Under an independence model, the expected overlap is \(1,000\times0.6\times0.5=300\). Independence is an added assumption about how the flags relate; it does not follow from their separate totals. The two feasible populations above show how different that relationship can be while leaving both totals unchanged. [B30-S1, B30-S3]

Simulating 10,000 independently assigned datasets explores that chosen model. More simulations can stabilize the model's average, but do not establish that the real administrative population follows the model or reveal its actual overlap. Even a stipulated actual overlap of exactly 300 would not satisfy the rule. [B30-S2, B30-S3]

The discussion group's 80 self-reports out of 100 respondents are not a random validation sample. Recruitment through an intensive-support channel, voluntary participation, and unverified flags prevent treating 80% as the population's overlap rate. The satisfaction report measures something else. Neither supplies the missing certified overlap information. [B30-S4]

## The minimum additional information

For this particular decision, the administrator needs only a trustworthy answer to:

> Using the same population, date, and flag definitions, do at least 301 distinct enrolled learners have both flags?

A certified **yes/no threshold result** is enough; an exact overlap count or complete cross-tabulation would provide more information than this decision requires. An authorized data custodian could evaluate the condition within the administrative system and release that aggregate result, without disclosing names, linked identifiers, or individual flag combinations.

Privacy restrictions do not erase the useful 100–500 bounds. They leave a narrower operational need: establish which side of the threshold the actual population occupies, without collecting or releasing unnecessary personal details.
