The scene makes unanimity appear by converting agreement about a shared emergency into recorded agreement with a proposal whose content and participants remain uncertain. Its strongest political tension is that people really do need shelter, while the procedures for deciding what to do can exclude people from both agreement and protection. [B27-S1–B27-S3]

## From urgency to apparent approval

The YES cards make assent visible before its meaning has been established. The actors place them on the table, but the cards were already waiting on their chairs: the scene does not show them choosing YES from available alternatives. The closed cupboard labeled OTHER CARDS suggests that alternatives exist while keeping them out of use. This supports a reading of constrained participation without fixing what every card must symbolize. [B27-S1]

The Clerk begins with the broad claim that “something must be done.” The Carpenter's “I agree that the roof—” could have specified the object or limits of agreement, but the bell interrupts him. “Agreement recorded” turns that unfinished statement into a completed administrative act. Later, agreeing that they “cannot wait all winter” establishes urgency, yet the Clerk records it with the same formula. Repetition gives the record a stable wording while the objects of agreement keep changing. Neither statement establishes approval of every part of the motion. [B27-S1, B27-S2]

The motion's journey from repairing the roof to removing the east wall makes that distinction consequential. Rain prevents the audience from hearing three intervening lines. We therefore cannot establish whether the ending follows sensibly from the beginning, changes the proposal radically, or contains qualifications. The Teacher's wet page further obstructs access to the wording. The Clerk's remark about affording only the dry part presents incomplete access as a practical necessity, but does not resolve what the participants have actually approved. [B27-S1, B27-S2]

## What the minutes count

“All voices present agreed” sounds comprehensive, but its scope depends on which voices count and when “present” is measured. The five original actors change chairs as the Clerk reads, while the child stays still. Occupied seats cease to offer a stable picture of membership, and the Teacher's “Present when?” exposes the missing time boundary in the official sentence. [B27-S3]

“No objection was entered” is narrower than “nobody objected” or “everyone consented.” It describes what reached the record. The interrupted statements, obstructed reading, and inaccessible cards give the audience reasons to question whether the process could adequately register reservations. They do not prove that every participant secretly opposed the motion. [B27-S1–B27-S3]

The child's blank card covering a YES breaks the visible pattern of assent without supplying an explicit NO. It could suggest withheld agreement, an unavailable form of participation, or resistance to the offered terms. The script leaves that gesture open. Likewise, the empty chairs invite questions about absent or unrepresented people without identifying a single fixed constituency for them. [B27-S2, B27-S3]

## Exclusion without a complete account of blame

A strong reading sees procedural exclusion: the Clerk closes discussion through interruption and recording, the alternative cards remain inaccessible, and the minutes present a finished consensus despite incomplete information. But the production notes resist reducing all this to one knowingly corrupt official. The Clerk must not sneer, and the rain must remain a real problem for everyone. His participation beneath the lifted table places him within the shared vulnerability. [B27-S1–B27-S4]

The Treasurer's key could make her an agent of obstruction, but the water beneath the cupboard could also make her hesitation reasonable. The performed version leaves the danger and her motive unsettled. A discarded draft calling her dishonest does not establish dishonesty in this scene, and the publisher's promotional description cannot erase those performed ambiguities. [B27-S2, B27-S4]

## Who finally receives shelter

Lifting the table creates an actual cooperative response to the rain, but its distribution matters: the original actors, including the Clerk, shelter together while the child stands outside. The falling YES cards separate practical cooperation from the earlier display of assent. Their common action cannot retrospectively establish that the motion was understood or freely approved. [B27-S3]

The Clerk's need for a sentence that would “hold water” links a defensible-looking record to the practical struggle to contain the rain. The child's question then tests the boundary of that collective “we”: who belongs even when their chair is empty, and whose need survives outside the group's solution? Addressing the audience makes that question extend beyond the officials onstage. Blackout withholds its answer. [B27-S3]

The scene consequently leaves both practical and political questions active: what the unheard lines said, whether the cupboard could safely be opened, what a fair decision procedure would require, and how shelter could include those outside. The script shows no final vote that would settle these issues, and the missing vote or wording should not be supplied by interpretation. [B27-S2–B27-S4]
