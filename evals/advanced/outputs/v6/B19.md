A starting cause can change a device so that a different process keeps it active afterward. Here, S starts A; the proposed A–B feedback maintains activity; and old reporter R can remain visible even after activity stops. Those are three different roles. [B19-S1]

### Starting the switch and keeping it on

The device already contains inactive A. S activates it, active A promotes B production, and B helps keep A active. This is **positive feedback**: activity produces something that reinforces that activity. It does not create A and B from nothing or require S to remain present. [B19-S1]

The direct A measurements support persistence after the starting signal: stimulated devices remained highly active for two hours after S removal, while unstimulated devices stayed inactive during the observed interval. S was therefore required to start these devices from the stated resting preparation under the tested interventions. That does not rule out an untested starting route. [B19-S2]

Deleting B preserved the early response to S but eliminated the observed persistence after S removal. Controls showed unchanged initial inactive A and S sensor amounts, making those particular explanations for the result less plausible. This supports a requirement for B for sustained activity under these conditions. However, deletion before stimulation cannot distinguish B's role in **establishing** a lasting state from its role in **continually maintaining** that state, and it does not prove every detail of the proposed feedback mechanism. [B19-S2]

### A lingering signal is not necessarily ongoing activity

R is accumulated fluorescent material, not an instantaneous reading of A. If production stops completely, three ten-minute halvings give:

\[
100\text{ units}\times(1/2)^{30/10}=12.5\text{ units}.
\]

The sequence is 100, 50, 25, then 12.5 units. A fractional number of fluorescence units is entirely compatible with the stated rule; there is no requirement that the measured amount be an integer. [B19-S1]

The observed 13 units is 0.5 unit above that prediction. Its numerical proximity shows why residual fluorescence cannot establish continued feedback. But the packet supplies no measurement precision or uncertainty range, so we cannot declare the difference statistically meaningful, attribute it to rounding, or establish exact agreement. [B19-S3]

Nor does the decline prove that the inhibitor directly inhibited A. Stopping reporter production was validated in a cell-free assay, not established inside this complete device. Even if production stopped inside it, the inhibitor could affect reporter production without directly stopping A. This batch has neither direct A measurements nor a characterization of the inhibitor's other effects. [B19-S3]

### What the next interventions would clarify

Removing B **after** activity is established would more directly test whether continued B is needed for maintenance. If verified B removal caused direct A activity to fall relative to appropriate controls, that would support an ongoing maintenance role. Measuring newly made R separately from old R, alongside A activity, would distinguish continued production from lingering fluorescence. These experiments are proposed, not completed. [B19-S4]

The demonstrated persistence lasts two hours. Unlimited memory, independence from energy or material supplies, and a specific intracellular inhibitor mechanism remain unestablished. A packaging award supplies evidence for none of those claims. [B19-S3; B19-S4]
