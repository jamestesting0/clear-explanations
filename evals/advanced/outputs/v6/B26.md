**Policy Clean has the highest total social surplus in these stipulated outcomes: 920 currency units, compared with 900 in the baseline and 700 under Policy Tax.** Tax collection and higher sales receipts are not themselves additions to the island's resources; payments between island residents redistribute purchasing power. [B26-S1–B26-S3]

## Who receives what

All figures below are totals for the period's 100 units. Buyers receive a stipulated total benefit of \(100\times22=2,200\), regardless of the observed price. [B26-S1]

Three accounting concepts keep the comparison clear:

- **Buyer surplus** is buyers' benefit minus what they pay: \(100\times(22-\text{price})\).
- **Producer contribution before common fixed costs** is gross receipts minus tax remittances and production resource costs. It is not gross sales revenue, and common fixed costs would still have to be deducted to obtain profit after those costs.
- **Council net receipts or costs** are tax receipts minus the policy's administration or monitoring cost. Existing funds used for monitoring still have an opportunity cost; they are not free resources. [B26-S1–B26-S3]

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Buyers' payments / gross sales receipts | 1,500 | 2,000 | 1,700 |
| Buyer surplus | 700 | 200 | 500 |
| Tax remitted to council | 0 | 500 | 0 |
| Producer contribution | 500 | 500 | 500 |
| Council net receipts or costs | 0 | 300 | −80 |

For example, the Tax producer receives 2,000 from buyers, remits 500, and uses 1,000 in production resources, leaving a contribution of 500. Its gross receipts rise by 500 relative to baseline, but its contribution does not rise. Under Clean, receipts of 1,700 less production costs of 1,200 also leave 500. [B26-S2, B26-S3]

## Why the two ways of counting agree

Distributional accounting adds buyer surplus, producer contribution, and council net receipts, then subtracts nuisance damage:

- Baseline: (700+500+0-300=900).
- Tax: (200+500+300-300=700).
- Clean: (500+500-80-0=920).

Product payments cancel between buyers and producers. Tax payments cancel between producers and the council. The remaining terms are benefits and real costs, so a direct calculation gives exactly the same results:

\[
\text{Social surplus}=\text{buyer benefit}-\text{production cost}-\text{nuisance damage}-\text{administration or monitoring cost}.
\]

- Baseline: (2,200-1,000-300-0=900).
- Tax: (2,200-1,000-300-200=700).
- Clean: (2,200-1,200-0-80=920). [B26-S1–B26-S3]

The 500 in tax revenue must not be added again as a separate social gain. Likewise, when council receipts have already been reduced by administration costs, those costs must not be subtracted a second time in the distributional sum.

## What the comparison establishes

Tax lowers social surplus by 200 relative to baseline because it consumes 200 in administration while leaving quantity, production costs, and nuisance damage unchanged. Buyers lose 500 in surplus; producer contribution stays unchanged; the council gains 300 net. This tax does not reduce nuisance in the supplied scenario. [B26-S2]

Clean raises social surplus by 20 relative to baseline: avoiding 300 in nuisance costs more than offsets the extra 200 in production resources and 80 in monitoring. Buyers' additional 200 in payments does reduce their surplus, but that payment is not itself an additional social cost. The real production cost increase is counted separately. Clean exceeds Tax by 220 in total surplus. [B26-S2, B26-S3]

The equal weighting is a substantive value judgment: one currency unit of benefit or cost receives the same weight regardless of which resident gains or loses, or that person's initial resources. A higher total therefore does not establish that every affected group benefits or that the distribution is acceptable. Clean's buyers and council bear losses relative to baseline while residents avoid nuisance damage. [B26-S1–B26-S3]

These outcomes rank the three stipulated scenarios; they do not establish an optimal future tax rate. That would require evidence about how quantities, prices, production methods, damage, and administration costs respond to other rates. The packet supplies no such responses or additional future benefits, and industrial heritage publicity does not supply them. [B26-S3, B26-S4]
