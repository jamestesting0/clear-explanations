**The window emits `12`, then a replacement total of `15`.** Its state is deleted at step 7, and step 8's amount `4` is dropped and counted. Neither the first emission nor deletion establishes that no more relevant events exist; those actions follow the protocol's chosen progress and acceptance rules. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

## Watermark and window trace

The watermark is the protocol's event-time progress marker. Each active partition proposes its maximum observed timestamp minus 10; the global watermark takes the minimum of those proposals but never moves backward. A data message is judged against the **watermark before that message updates any progress information**. [A08-RULES]

Initially both partition maxima are zero, both proposals are −10, and the global watermark is −10.

| Step | Partition proposals after the step: p0, p1 | Global watermark, before → after | Fate of `[0,60)` | Emission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: p0 data `(20, 5)` | 10, −10 | −10 → −10 | Accept 5; total 5 | None |
| 2: p1 data `(50, 7)` | 10, 40 | −10 → 10 | Accept 7; total 12 | None |
| 3: p0 heartbeat 80 | 70, 40 | 10 → 40 | Retain total 12 | None |
| 4: p1 heartbeat 75 | 70, 65 | 40 → 65 | Emit initial result; retain state | **12** |
| 5: p0 data `(40, 3)` | 70, 65 | 65 → 65 | Accept 3; total becomes 15 | **15**, replacing 12 |
| 6: p1 idle | 70, excluded | 65 → 70 | Retain total 15 | None |
| 7: p0 heartbeat 100 | 90, excluded | 70 → 90 | Delete window state permanently | None |
| 8: p1 data `(55, 4)` | 90, 65 | 90 → 90 | Drop record; state stays deleted | None |

The table is calculated from [A08-RULES] and [A08-INPUT].

At **step 4**, the watermark first reaches or exceeds 60, so the initial total is emitted. It is still below 80, so the protocol retains the state and permits corrections. A heartbeat can trigger that initial emission; the rule against control-generated corrections does not prohibit the initial result. [A08-RULES]

At **step 5**, event time 40 belongs to `[0,60)`. Although it is behind the current watermark of 65, the acceptance cutoff is 80. The amount is therefore added before any watermark recomputation, and the already emitted window immediately produces replacement total `12 + 3 = 15`. p0's maximum remains 80, so its proposal remains 70 and the global watermark remains 65. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

At **step 6**, excluding p1 removes its proposal of 65 from the minimum. The global watermark can advance to p0's proposal of 70. At step 7 it advances to 90, crossing the deletion threshold. This is how the idle rule allows an active partition to make progress without waiting indefinitely for another partition. Neither control produces an additional correction. [A08-RULES]

At **step 8**, the amount is judged against the existing watermark of 90 and is dropped. Reactivation brings p1 back into future minimum calculations, but its maximum remains 75: receiving timestamp 55 cannot reduce that maximum. The recomputed minimum is 65, and monotonicity preserves `max(90, 65) = 90`. Reactivation therefore cannot lower the watermark, restore deleted state, or retroactively accept the dropped amount. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

There is **one dropped record**, carrying amount **4**. The last emitted total remains **15**, not 19. With no further messages, there are no further emissions. [A08-DOWNSTREAM]

## Why downstream addition is incorrect

The emitted numbers are successive **complete totals for the same window**, not separate contributions. Adding them gives `12 + 15 = 27`, already wrong without transport retries. Redelivery can increase that error further. [A08-RULES; A08-DOWNSTREAM]

A safe output contract would give every window a stable identity and each successive result a strictly increasing version. For example:

```text
window=[0,60), version=1, total=12
window=[0,60), version=2, total=15
```

A retransmission must retain the original window, version, and total. A transport retry must not invent another version. The producer must preserve these identities across recovery.

The consumer stores `(version, total)` for each window and processes each message atomically:

- **Higher version:** replace the stored total and version.
- **Same version, same total:** ignore the duplicate.
- **Lower version:** ignore an obsolete result, including an old message redelivered after a correction.
- **Same version, different total:** report a conflict rather than silently choosing a payload.

Receiving version 2 before version 1 is then safe: the consumer stores 15 and later ignores version 1. If it also maintains a sum across windows, it updates that sum by the difference between the new and previously stored contribution, in the same transaction, rather than adding the entire incoming total.

The consumer's remembered version must survive for as long as an old output can be redelivered; deleting it too early would let an obsolete message appear new. Window identifier and total alone do not supply the ordering information needed to distinguish a delayed earlier result from a genuine later correction. [A08-DOWNSTREAM]

## What the late-data policy gives up

The protocol chooses finite retention and continued progress over accepting every event that might eventually arrive. Keeping the state while the watermark is below 80 allows some late corrections. Deleting it at 80 or above prevents indefinitely retaining this window, but sacrifices relevant records that arrive afterward, including the amount 4 here. Excluding idle partitions can bring that cutoff forward relative to waiting for them. [A08-RULES]

The first emission means “the watermark reached 60.” Deletion means “our acceptance deadline has passed and we will no longer revise this window.” Neither means the physical world cannot produce another event with a timestamp in `[0,60)`. Wall-clock time contributes nothing to these rules, and a disconnected partition's eventual return does not override the expressly chosen cutoff. [A08-RULES; A08-DOWNSTREAM]
