The model predicts **lower median duration with acceleration throughout the observed 100–200 MiB range, but not lower arithmetic-mean duration throughout that range**. It describes associations between mode and duration; it does not establish the effect of switching the feature on. [A11-MODEL; A11-STUDY]

## What the coefficients mean

Write the fitted mean of log-duration as \(\mu\). For standard mode, \(\mu_0=4+0.20x\); for accelerated mode, \(\mu_1=3.90+0.05x\). These are predictions of **natural-log duration**, with duration measured in seconds. The coefficients are not seconds or already-converted percentages. [A11-MODEL]

Using the supplied identity, the conditional median is \(e^\mu\). Thus the accelerated-to-standard median ratio at the same size is

\[
\frac{\operatorname{median}(T\mid x,z=1)}{\operatorname{median}(T\mid x,z=0)}
=e^{-0.10-0.15x}.
\]

| File size | Standard median | Accelerated median | Accelerated / standard | Median change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB: \(x=0\) | 54.60 s | 49.40 s | 0.9048 | 9.52% lower |
| 200 MiB: \(x=1\) | 66.69 s | 51.94 s | 0.7788 | 22.12% lower |

These are calculated from [A11-MODEL] using [A11-IDENTITY]. At 100 MiB, the median difference is about 5.20 seconds, not 0.10 seconds. The absolute difference also changes with size.

**The size slopes differ by mode.** Adding 100 MiB increases \(x\) by one. Within the modeled range, this multiplies median duration by \(e^{0.20}=1.2214\) in standard mode, a 22.14% increase, and by \(e^{0.05}=1.0513\) in accelerated mode, a 5.13% increase. Because each mode's residual variance is constant across size, its arithmetic mean has the same within-mode multiplier. [A11-MODEL; A11-IDENTITY]

The interaction coefficient \(-0.15\) reduces the log-duration size slope from 0.20 to 0.05 **per additional 100 MiB**. Equivalently, it multiplies the accelerated-to-standard duration ratio by \(e^{-0.15}=0.8607\) across that increase. It is neither a 15% saving nor a per-MiB coefficient. [A11-MODEL]

## Why the means can have a different ordering

Exponentiating \(\mu\) gives the median. The arithmetic mean also depends on the spread of log-duration:

\[
E[T\mid x,z=0]=e^{4+0.20x+0.40^2/2}=e^{4.08+0.20x},
\]
\[
E[T\mid x,z=1]=e^{3.90+0.05x+0.80^2/2}=e^{4.22+0.05x}.
\]

Consequently, the accelerated-to-standard **mean ratio** is

\[
e^{-0.10-0.15x+(0.80^2-0.40^2)/2}=e^{0.14-0.15x}.
\]

| File size | Standard mean | Accelerated mean | Accelerated / standard | Mean change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB | 59.15 s | 68.03 s | 1.1503 | 15.03% higher |
| 200 MiB | 72.24 s | 71.52 s | 0.9900 | 1.00% lower |

Calculations use [A11-MODEL; A11-IDENTITY]; displayed durations are rounded. The larger residual spread in accelerated mode produces a longer upper tail of durations, raising its arithmetic mean relative to its median. At 100 MiB, this outweighs the lower median. The fitted means are equal at approximately 193.33 MiB, where \(0.14-0.15x=0\).

The variance correction belongs to calculating the distribution's mean; it is not an additional coefficient or a correction to each individual observation. For the product team, the median describes the middle request, while expected total machine-seconds for a specified batch is the sum of its requests' arithmetic means. The batch's file-size mix therefore matters. [A11-IDENTITY; A11-STUDY]

## Limits and a more direct study

Users selected their mode, and unrecorded graphics-card quality, model complexity, and retry history could affect both selection and duration. The fitted comparison cannot separate acceleration's causal effect from these differences. Nor does the packet support extending the size relationship below 100 or above 200 MiB. [A11-STUDY; A11-MODEL]

A randomized experiment assigning eligible requests to standard or accelerated mode would address switching more directly. Balance assignment within file-size and hardware groups, record workload complexity and retries, and compare both median duration and arithmetic-mean machine-seconds. If the same workloads are run in both modes, randomize their order and standardize warm-up conditions so that running second does not systematically favor one mode.
