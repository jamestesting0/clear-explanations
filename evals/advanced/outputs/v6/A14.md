## Two separate spreadsheet errors

The spreadsheet's unweighted mean is \((1000+2000+1000+0)/4=1000\) W. This gives equal weight to four readings even though their durations inside the target interval are 30, 60, 30, and 0 seconds. Simply removing the endpoint would still leave unequal interval durations needing appropriate weights. [A14-SPREADSHEET; A14-SAMPLES]

Multiplying that incorrect mean by 120 s gives 120,000 J. Dividing by 1,000 gives **120 kJ**, not 120 kWh. Conversion from joules to kilowatt-hours requires division by \(3600\times1000=3{,}600{,}000\). Fixing only this divisor yields \(120{,}000/3{,}600{,}000=0.033333\ldots\) kWh, still one-third below the correct analog total because the averaging error remains. [A14-SPREADSHEET; A14-SAMPLES]

## What trapezoidal integration assumes

Trapezoids use the mean of the two endpoint powers for each interval:

\[
E_{\mathrm{trap}}
=\frac{1000+2000}{2}(30)
+\frac{2000+1000}{2}(60)
+\frac{1000+0}{2}(30)
=150{,}000\ \mathrm{J}.
\]

The three contributions are 45,000, 90,000, and 15,000 J. The total is **41.6667 Wh**, or approximately **0.041667 kWh**. [A14-SAMPLES]

This calculation assumes power changes linearly between readings. It therefore lets the 120 s reading influence the preceding interval by assuming a gradual decline to zero. The supplied model instead holds power at 1,000 W throughout [90, 120), then changes it at 120 s. Rectangles are exact for that model; trapezoids replace its physical assumption rather than improve its numerical accuracy. [A14-CALIBRATION; A14-SAMPLES]

## Comparison with the cumulative register

The displayed increase is \(12.052-12.000=0.052\) kWh. Each endpoint can differ from its true reading by up to 0.001 kWh. With no assumption that their errors cancel, the difference can be wrong by up to \(0.001+0.001=0.002\) kWh. Therefore the true increase is bounded by:

\[
0.052\pm0.002
=\boxed{[0.050,\ 0.054]\ \mathrm{kWh}}.
\]

The observed 0.002 kWh discrepancy is exactly the allowed worst-case difference error. Agreement is possible at the boundary, with opposite endpoint errors at their limits. This is a possible-error bound, **not a confidence interval or a probability statement**; no error distribution was supplied. [A14-REGISTER]

In a real installation, correct arithmetic would remain conditional on the calibration and hold model. If either were approximate, its additional error would need assessment before interpreting the comparison. Check calibration against known loads, use sufficiently frequent measurements to investigate changes between samples, and verify that both instruments cover the same load and time interval. The present calculation establishes what the supplied model predicts; it does not independently validate that model against the physical load or justify replacing an instrument.
