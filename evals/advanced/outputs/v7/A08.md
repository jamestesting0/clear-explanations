**The window emits 12, then a replacement total of 15.** Its state is deleted at step 7, and the amount 4 at step 8 is dropped and counted. The first emission is preliminary under these rules, regardless of the wall clock. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

## Step-by-step trace

A partition’s proposed watermark is its largest observed timestamp minus 10. The global watermark takes the minimum across active partitions, but never moves backward. For data messages, acceptance uses the watermark **before** that message updates the partition’s timestamp.

Initially both partition maxima are 0, the global watermark is −10, and the window has emitted nothing.

| Step | Partition maxima after the step: p0, p1 | Active partitions | Global watermark, before → after | Fate of [0,60) and output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: p0 data at 20, amount 5 | 20, 0 | p0, p1 | −10 → −10 | Accept 5; stored total 5. No output. |
| 2: p1 data at 50, amount 7 | 20, 50 | p0, p1 | −10 → 10 | Accept 7; stored total 12. No output. |
| 3: p0 heartbeat at 80 | 80, 50 | p0, p1 | 10 → 40 | Retain total 12. No output. |
| 4: p1 heartbeat at 75 | 80, 75 | p0, p1 | 40 → 65 | First crossing of 60: emit **12** and retain state. |
| 5: p0 data at 40, amount 3 | 80, 75 | p0, p1 | 65 → 65 | Accept 3; immediately emit replacement **15**. |
| 6: p1 becomes idle | 80, 75 | p0 | 65 → 70 | Retain total 15. No output. |
| 7: p0 heartbeat at 100 | 100, 75 | p0 | 70 → 90 | Delete window state permanently. No output. |
| 8: p1 data at 55, amount 4 | 100, 75 | p0, p1 | 90 → 90 | Drop amount 4; increment the dropped-record count by one. State remains deleted. |

These values follow from the supplied arrival order and rules. In particular, the heartbeat at step 4 produces proposals of 70 and 65, so the global watermark reaches 65 and triggers the initial result. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

### Why step 5 changes an emitted result

Event time 40 belongs to [0,60). Although the watermark is already past the window’s end, it is still below the deletion threshold of 80. The rules therefore accept the amount 3 and replace the previously emitted total with 12 + 3 = 15.

The partition maximum stays at 80 because maxima never decrease. This data message consequently leaves the global watermark at 65. Its correction comes from accepting the data, not from advancing the watermark.

### Why idleness allows progress

Before step 6, p1’s proposed watermark of 65 holds the minimum down. Excluding p1 lets the global watermark follow p0: first to 70, then to 90 when p0’s maximum reaches 100. Without that exclusion, p1’s unchanged proposal would hold the global watermark at 65 through step 7.

This is an explicit decision to let an inactive partition stop delaying cleanup. It does not establish that the partition has no older data left to deliver. [A08-RULES]

### What reactivation can reverse

Step 8 reactivates p1, so it participates in subsequent watermark calculations. It cannot undo this window’s deletion or lower the global watermark.

The amount is judged first against the existing watermark of 90 and is dropped. The event timestamp 55 does not lower p1’s previous maximum of 75. Recomputing the active minimum gives min(100 − 10, 75 − 10) = 65, but monotonicity preserves the existing global watermark of 90. Reactivation restores participation, not earlier window state. [A08-RULES; A08-INPUT]

## The downstream consumer must replace totals

Adding every emitted number is incorrect. Even without redelivery, it would store 12 + 15 = **27**, although the second message says that this window’s current total is **15**. Redelivery could inflate that incorrect sum further. The messages represent complete replacement totals, not increments. [A08-RULES; A08-DOWNSTREAM]

One suitable output contract is:

| Window identity | Version | Replacement total |
|---|---:|---:|
| This metric’s [0,60) window | 1 | 12 |
| This metric’s [0,60) window | 2 | 15 |

The window identity must include whatever stream, metric, or other grouping distinguishes windows. Versions must increase for genuinely new results; retransmitting the same result must preserve its identity, version, and total.

The consumer should persist the latest version and total for each window and compare/update them atomically:

- A greater version replaces the saved total.
- The same version with the same total is a duplicate and does nothing.
- A smaller version is stale and does nothing.
- The same version with a different total is a conflict to investigate.

This also prevents a delayed duplicate of version 1 from overwriting version 2. If the database maintains a grand total across windows, update that grand total by the difference between the newly accepted total and the saved total, in the same transaction; do not add each incoming total.

The producer must preserve version identities across its own retries or recovery, and the consumer must retain its version information for the full possible redelivery period. If redelivery has no time limit, simply deleting that information after a short timeout loses the duplicate protection.

## What the policy trades away

The policy allows an initial answer at watermark 60, accepts corrections while the watermark is below 80, and bounds how long this window’s state is retained as the watermark advances. Excluding idle partitions helps reach those thresholds. The cost is that relevant records arriving after cleanup are discarded: the supplied amount 4 is such a record. The four supplied amounts sum to 19, but the accepted total reported by this protocol remains 15.

The first emission does not mean no more relevant data will arrive; step 5 demonstrates that. State deletion means the system will no longer incorporate such data, not that the physical world cannot produce or deliver it. There is no extra “final total” emission at deletion in the supplied rules. The manager’s wall-clock argument changes none of these decisions, and keeping every window forever would be a different policy from the one specified. [A08-RULES; A08-DOWNSTREAM]
