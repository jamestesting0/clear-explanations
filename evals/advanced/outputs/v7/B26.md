**Total social surplus is 900 in the baseline, 700 under Policy Tax, and 920 under Policy Clean.** These are calculated gains after real resource costs and nuisance damage, not amounts of money collected. Under the stipulated outcomes and equal weighting, Clean has the highest total; Tax's 500 of revenue does not constitute an additional social benefit. [B26-S1, B26-S2, B26-S3]

## Separate the quantities

All figures below are currency units for the whole 100-unit period. Purchasers' total benefit is (100\times22=2{,}200) in every scenario. [B26-S1]

- **Buyer surplus** is purchasers' stipulated benefit minus what they pay.
- **Producer contribution before common fixed costs** is sales revenue minus tax remitted and production resource costs. It is not gross sales or a complete measure of profit after all fixed costs.
- **Council net receipts or costs** are tax receipts minus administration or monitoring costs.
- **Nuisance damage** is a real loss to other residents, even though nobody charges the producer for it.
- **Total social surplus** adds buyer surplus, producer contribution, and council net receipts, then subtracts nuisance damage. Common fixed costs are omitted because they are identical across scenarios. [B26-S1, B26-S2, B26-S3]

| Period total | Baseline | Policy Tax | Policy Clean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Buyers' payments / producers' gross sales | 1,500 | 2,000 | 1,700 |
| Buyer surplus | 700 | 200 | 500 |
| Tax remitted by producers | 0 | 500 | 0 |
| Production resource cost | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,200 |
| Producer contribution | 500 | 500 | 500 |
| Council net receipts or costs | 0 | 300 | −80 |
| Nuisance damage, to be subtracted | 300 | 300 | 0 |
| **Total social surplus** | **900** | **700** | **920** |

For example, under Tax the producer receives 2,000 from buyers, remits 500, and incurs 1,000 in production costs, leaving 500. The council receives 500 but uses 200 of real resources administering the policy, leaving net receipts of 300. Under Clean, the council incurs an 80 monitoring cost from existing funds; the absence of a new tax does not make that resource cost disappear. [B26-S2, B26-S3]

## Reconcile money flows with real benefits and costs

Distributional accounting gives:

- Baseline: (700+500+0-300=900).
- Tax: (200+500+300-300=700).
- Clean: (500+500-80-0=920).

Independently, subtract real production costs, nuisance damage, and administration from total purchaser benefit:

- Baseline: (2{,}200-1{,}000-300-0=900).
- Tax: (2{,}200-1{,}000-300-200=700).
- Clean: (2{,}200-1{,}200-0-80=920).

Both calculations use the same stipulated outcomes. Product payments cancel between buyers and producers; tax payments cancel between producers and the council. Counting those payments again as social benefits would count transfers twice. Production, damage, and administration remain because they consume resources or impose losses. [B26-S1, B26-S2, B26-S3]

## What the comparisons establish

**Tax reduces total surplus by 200 relative to baseline.** Buyers lose 500 of surplus, producers' contribution is unchanged, and the council gains 300 net. Nuisance remains 300 because quantity and production method remain unchanged. The net loss is the administration cost. Neither gross sales increasing by 500 nor tax collection of 500 establishes an income or social gain of that amount. No additional benefit from future spending is specified. [B26-S2]

**Clean increases total surplus by 20 relative to baseline:** avoiding 300 of damage outweighs the additional 200 of production costs and 80 of monitoring costs. Buyers lose 200 of surplus, the council incurs 80, affected residents gain 300 through avoided damage, and producer contribution is unchanged. Higher buyer payments therefore do not by themselves establish a social loss. Clean's total exceeds Tax's by 220. [B26-S2, B26-S3]

Equal weighting is a value judgment: a currency unit of valued benefit or cost counts equally whoever receives or bears it. An aggregate gain does not establish that every group benefits, that losers are compensated, or that the distribution is acceptable. [B26-S1]

These scenarios also do not identify an optimal future tax rate. They provide no response of output, production method, prices, or damage to other rates, and no additional innovation or employment benefit to add. The ranking applies to the stipulated outcomes; it cannot establish how another policy setting or future year would behave. [B26-S2, B26-S3, B26-S4]
