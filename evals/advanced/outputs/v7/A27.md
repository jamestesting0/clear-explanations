**After minute 2, the degradation probability is approximately 0.09031, so immediate inspection costs more than the expected current loss. After minute 3, it is approximately 0.5457, so inspection is justified under the supplied costs.** These probabilities must be calculated by allowing the machine's state to change before each reading. ## Predict, then update

Let \(p_{t-1}\) be the probability of degradation after processing all readings through the previous minute. Let \(q_t\) be the probability for the current minute before its reading.

The transition rule gives

\[
q_t=0.80p_{t-1}+0.05(1-p_{t-1})
=0.05+0.75p_{t-1}.
\]

The first term accounts for a previously degraded machine remaining degraded; the second accounts for a previously normal machine becoming degraded. This prediction uses the previous updated probability, beginning with \(p_0=0.10\). [A27-STATE]

Next, incorporate the current detector result. For an alarm,

\[
p_t=\frac{0.90q_t}{0.90q_t+0.10(1-q_t)};
\]

for a quiet reading,

\[
p_t=\frac{0.10q_t}{0.10q_t+0.90(1-q_t)}.
\]

Each denominator includes both ways to obtain the observed reading: from a degraded machine and from a normal one. [A27-SENSOR]

Using full precision between steps gives:

| Minute | Observation | Before the reading, \(q_t\) | After the reading, \(p_t\) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | Alarm | 0.1250 | 0.5625 |
| 2 | Quiet | 0.4719 | 0.09031 |
| 3 | Alarm | 0.1177 | 0.5457 |

For example, the minute-1 prediction is \(0.80(0.10)+0.05(0.90)=0.125\). Updating it with the alarm gives \(0.1125/(0.1125+0.0875)=0.5625\). Before minute 2, transition again: \(0.05+0.75(0.5625)=0.471875\). Its quiet reading then gives \(0.0471875/(0.0471875+0.4753125)\approx0.0903110\). The minute-3 calculation follows the same two steps. These are derived from [A27-STATE], [A27-SENSOR], and [A27-OBSERVATIONS].

With **no intervention**, the minute-4 prediction before its reading is

\[
q_4=0.05+0.75\left(\frac{35433}{64936}\right)
\approx\boxed{0.4592}.
\]

Here \(35433/64936\) is the unrounded minute-3 updated probability. This minute-4 number is a prediction for a later state, conditional on leaving the machine untreated.

## Where the engineers change the model

The first engineer's calculation is

\[
\frac{0.10(0.90)(0.10)(0.90)}
{0.10(0.90)(0.10)(0.90)+0.90(0.10)(0.90)(0.10)}
=0.50.
\]

That calculation treats every reading as evidence about one fixed state. It omits transitions, including the transition before minute 1. Under that different model, an alarm's likelihood ratio of 9 and a quiet reading's likelihood ratio of \(1/9\) do cancel. Under the supplied model, readings concern different states linked by transitions, so this cancellation argument does not apply. Conditional independence given the entire state sequence does not make the readings independent after those states are left unknown. [A27-STATE] [A27-SENSOR]

The second engineer discards earlier evidence by restoring 0.10 before every reading. The stated 0.10 is the time-zero probability, not a standing replacement for each predicted probability. Earlier observations influence the current prediction through the previous updated probability. Even the first reading should start from 0.125 after its transition, not 0.10.

## Turn the current probability into a decision

For a current degradation probability \(p\), immediate inspection costs **3**, while operating without inspection has expected current loss

\[
20p+0(1-p)=20p.
\]

Inspection therefore minimizes the stated expected cost when

\[
3<20p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p>0.15.
\]

At exactly 0.15, the two actions have equal expected cost. Below it, operate without inspection. [A27-DECISION]

| Information available at decision | Degradation probability | Expected loss without inspection | Decision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| After minute 2 | 0.09031 | ≈ 1.806 cost units | Operate without inspection |
| After minute 3 | 0.5457 | ≈ 10.91 cost units | Inspect for 3 cost units |

The manager is right that a probability alone does not specify an action. The supplied consequences supply the missing decision rule: compare the certain inspection cost with the expected loss. These are decisions for the current minute under the stated cost model, not a policy optimizing future operation. They evaluate the supplied history, which explicitly contains no maintenance intervention.

## Available information and time horizons

**Filtering** means estimating the current state using readings available through that moment. The minute-2 decision therefore uses only alarm, quiet. Using minute 3's alarm to revise the estimate of minute 2 would be **retrospective inference**, often called smoothing: estimating a past state using later evidence. It can be useful for a later investigation, but it cannot describe what the operator knew when taking the minute-2 action. [A27-OBSERVATIONS]

Likewise, \(q_4\approx0.4592\) cannot replace \(p_3\approx0.5457\) in the minute-3 decision. The loss in that decision depends on degradation **at minute 3**, whereas \(q_4\) includes another opportunity for the state to change. It also assumes no inspection intervenes. If the minute-3 inspection is performed and leaves the machine normal as specified, the next minute's prediction instead starts from that known normal state and is 0.05. [A27-STATE] [A27-DECISION]
