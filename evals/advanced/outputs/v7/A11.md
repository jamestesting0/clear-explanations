Acceleration has a lower fitted **median** duration at both 100 and 200 MiB, but its fitted **arithmetic mean** is higher at 100 MiB and slightly lower at 200 MiB. The coefficients describe changes in log duration, and this observational study does not establish what switching the feature on would cause. [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY] [A11-STUDY]

### Median durations and the interaction

Let `mu` denote the fitted mean of `ln(T)`, with duration `T` measured in seconds. Substituting each mode into the model gives:

- Standard: `mu = 4.00 + 0.20x`.
- Accelerated: `mu = 3.90 + 0.05x`.

Because the conditional median is `exp(mu)`, the accelerated-to-standard median ratio at a fixed size is `exp(-0.10 - 0.15x)`. Here, `x=0` means 100 MiB and `x=1` means 200 MiB. Using the supplied model and identity: [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY]

| File size | Standard median | Accelerated median | Accelerated ÷ standard | Median reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB | 54.60 s | 49.40 s | 0.9048 | 9.52% |
| 200 MiB | 66.69 s | 51.94 s | 0.7788 | 22.12% |

Thus, the `-0.10` coefficient means a multiplicative median difference at **100 MiB**, not a saving of 0.10 seconds for every file. At 100 MiB the median difference is about 5.20 seconds; at 200 MiB it is about 14.75 seconds.

The size slope is also a change in log duration per **100 MiB**, not per MiB:

- Standard mode: adding 100 MiB multiplies the median by `exp(0.20) = 1.2214`, an increase of 22.14%.
- Accelerated mode: the interaction changes the slope to `0.20 - 0.15 = 0.05`. Adding 100 MiB multiplies the median by `exp(0.05) = 1.0513`, an increase of 5.13%.

The interaction therefore makes duration grow more slowly with size in accelerated mode. For each additional 100 MiB, it multiplies the accelerated-to-standard median ratio by `exp(-0.15) = 0.8607`, reducing that ratio by about 13.93%. It is not a further 15% saving for each MiB. These comparisons apply within the observed 100–200 MiB range. [A11-MODEL]

### Arithmetic means and batch consumption

Exponentiating `mu` gives the median, the middle of the duration distribution. The arithmetic mean—the expected duration relevant to total seconds across a large batch—requires the residual-variance correction. That correction is `0.40²/2 = 0.08` for standard mode and `0.80²/2 = 0.32` for accelerated mode. [A11-IDENTITY]

Consequently, the accelerated-to-standard mean ratio is

`exp[(-0.10 - 0.15x) + (0.32 - 0.08)] = exp(0.14 - 0.15x).`

| File size | Standard mean | Accelerated mean | Accelerated ÷ standard | Mean change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MiB | 59.15 s | 68.03 s | 1.1503 | 15.03% higher |
| 200 MiB | 72.24 s | 71.52 s | 0.9900 | 1.00% lower |

These are calculated from the supplied residual distributions. Accelerated mode has more spread in log duration; its long-duration tail raises its arithmetic mean more strongly. Its mean ratio is therefore its median ratio multiplied by `exp(0.24) ≈ 1.2712`. A lower median can coexist with a higher mean. The correction calculates a distributional mean; it is not an extra term to add to an individual observation's log duration. [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY]

### What the evidence supports

Users selected their own mode, and hardware quality, model complexity, and prior retries were unrecorded potential influences on both selection and duration. Conditioning on file size does not remove those explanations, so neither the negative coefficient nor the median comparisons prove a causal benefit. Nor does the packet support extrapolation below 100 or above 200 MiB. [A11-STUDY] [A11-MODEL]

A more direct study would randomly assign acceleration on or off for comparable workloads, balancing assignments within hardware and file-size groups. Repeated runs on the same machine and workload could provide useful comparisons if run order is randomized and warm-up and retry conditions are controlled. Prespecify both median duration and arithmetic mean duration: they answer the team's distinct questions about a typical request and expected batch consumption.
