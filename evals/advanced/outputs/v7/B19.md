A cause can start a process without having to remain present to keep it going. In this model, S starts A, the A–B feedback can sustain activity afterward, and old reporter R can remain visible even after that activity has stopped. These are three different roles. [B19-S1]

## Starting and maintaining the switch

The device initially contains inactive A. S activates it; active A promotes B production; B then helps keep A active. That return influence is **positive feedback**: activity produces something that supports more activity. It does not create the starting components from nothing, and it works only under suitable conditions. [B19-S1]

The direct A measurements distinguish this mechanism from a merely lingering fluorescent signal:

- After an S pulse, A remained highly active for two hours after S removal. Thus, continued S was unnecessary for the observed period of maintenance.
- Devices never given S remained inactive. S was required to start the standard preparation under the tested interventions; untested alternative starting routes remain possible.
- Devices lacking B still responded initially to S, but A returned to baseline after S removal. This supports a role for B in achieving persistent A activity, while showing that the early S response can occur without B. The controls address changes to the initial A supply and S sensor. [B19-S2]

Deleting B before stimulation does not, by itself, distinguish a role in *establishing* the persistent state from an ongoing role in *maintaining* an already-established state.

## What the remaining fluorescence means

R is a stock of previously produced reporter, not an instantaneous reading of A. If new production completely stops and the stated decay calibration applies, thirty minutes contains three ten-minute halving periods:

\[
R(30)=100\times(1/2)^3=12.5\text{ units}.
\]

The predicted sequence is 100, 50, 25, and 12.5 units at zero, ten, twenty, and thirty minutes. A fractional number of reporter units is valid; the prediction need not be an integer. [B19-S1]

The observed 13 units is 0.5 units above that conditional prediction. The values are not exactly equal, but the packet supplies no measurement precision or uncertainty with which to judge that discrepancy. We cannot declare it an established rounding effect, a statistically acceptable match, or a decisive refutation of the calibration. [B19-S3]

More importantly, neither analyst's causal conclusion follows:

- **Remaining fluorescence does not establish continuing feedback.** Old R is expected to remain even with no new production.
- **Declining fluorescence does not establish direct inhibition of A.** Reporter production could be blocked while A remained active; alternatively, the inhibitor could affect A or other parts of the complete device. Its suppression of reporter production was validated in a cell-free assay, while its effects in the complete device were not characterized. No direct A measurement was collected in this batch. [B19-S3]

## What the proposed experiments would resolve

Removing B only after activity is established would test whether ongoing B support is needed. If verified, selective B removal caused direct A activity to fall, compared with an otherwise matched control, that would strengthen the maintenance claim. Persistence after effective removal would weaken a claim that B must remain present throughout maintenance under those conditions. Measuring newly made R separately from old R, alongside direct A activity, would distinguish reporter persistence, reporter production, and switch activity. These experiments are planned, not completed. [B19-S4]

The demonstrated persistence lasts two hours. Neither indefinite operation nor the required energy and material supply was measured, so “perpetual memory” goes beyond the evidence. A packaging award adds no evidence about those questions. [B19-S4]
