Both runs reach `after-gather` with A still holding its 30-unit reservation: `available` is 70, `committed` is 0, and A is neither done nor cancelled. B's failure ends the caller's wait on `gather`; it does not end A. The subsequent decision to release A's gate or cancel and await A produces the two different final states. [A01-CODE]

## The output and its causes

For `scenario(False)`:

```text
after-gather {'available': 70, 'committed': 0} False False
final {'available': 70, 'committed': 30}
trace A:reserved,B:reserved,B:released,B:cleanup,caller:caught,A:committed,A:cleanup
```

For `scenario(True)`:

```text
after-gather {'available': 70, 'committed': 0} False False
final {'available': 100, 'committed': 0}
trace A:reserved,B:reserved,B:released,B:cleanup,caller:caught,A:released,A:cleanup,caller:cancel-observed
```

I ran the supplied program under Python 3.12.14 and obtained these outputs.

The common part of both runs is:

1. A subtracts 30, leaving 70 available, records `A:reserved`, and suspends at `gate.wait()`.
2. B subtracts 20, leaving 50 available, records `B:reserved`, and yields at `sleep(0)`.
3. B resumes and raises `ValueError`. Its handler restores its 20 units and records `B:released`; its `finally` records `B:cleanup`. B then finishes with that exception.
4. `gather` propagates the exception to the caller, which records `caller:caught`. A is still waiting for its gate, so the first print shows 70 available and no committed quantity. [A01-CODE]

In the first run, `gate.set()` lets A resume. A adds 30 to `committed`, records `A:committed`, runs its `finally`, and returns 30. Its reservation becomes committed rather than available again. Awaiting A finishes before the final print.

In the second run, `a.cancel()` requests cancellation. At A's suspended await, cancellation raises `asyncio.CancelledError`. A's `except BaseException` restores its 30 units and records `A:released`; `finally` records `A:cleanup`; the bare `raise` lets cancellation leave the task. The caller's `await a` raises that cancellation only after this unwinding has completed, and the caller records `caller:cancel-observed`. [A01-CODE]

A useful accounting equation throughout this example is:

```text
available + committed + outstanding uncommitted reservations = 100
```

At either first checkpoint, the terms are `70 + 0 + 30`. The shortfall in `available + committed` describes A's outstanding reservation; it is not evidence that B failed to release its own reservation. At either final checkpoint, no uncommitted reservation remains.

## What each mechanism guarantees

**`gather` propagates B's failure without automatically cancelling A.** With the default `return_exceptions=False`, the first raised exception can reach the caller while another supplied task remains unfinished. Catching that exception is therefore not a group rollback boundary. In this program, B's own exception handler performs B's release; `gather` performs no reservation accounting. A later cancellation of an already completed, failed `gather` would not fix this by cancelling its remaining children.

**Explicit cancellation requests unwinding; awaiting the task observes its completion.** Calling `a.cancel()` alone does not wait for cleanup. Here, awaiting A and catching its `CancelledError` puts the final print after A's release and cleanup. Cancellation does not skip exception handlers or `finally` blocks: the supplied trace directly demonstrates both running. Tasks can catch cancellation and behave differently, so the conclusion depends on A's shown handler re-raising it.

**The choice of exception class matters.** `asyncio.CancelledError` derives from `BaseException`, not `Exception`. Replacing this handler with `except Exception` would still release B's reservation for its `ValueError`, but would skip A's release on cancellation. A's `finally` would still record `A:cleanup`, and the caller would still observe cancellation, yet the cancellation run would finish with `available=70` and `committed=0`. Cleanup logging alone would conceal the unreleased 30 units.

Short examples that finish immediately after catching B's error can be misleading for another reason: `asyncio.run` cancels unfinished tasks while shutting down its event loop. That is a separate shutdown action, not sibling cancellation by `gather`. This example deliberately remains alive long enough to show the distinction. [A01-CODE, A01-NOTES]

## A concrete structure for the group requirement

Give one coordinator responsibility for the group's response and for a ledger of reservations. A suitable design for the in-memory model is:

1. **Prepare without committing.** Child tasks acquire and validate reservations, recording every acquired reservation in the coordinator's ledger before they next suspend. Track each reservation as uncommitted, released, or committed. Returning a preparation result to the coordinator does not send a success response to a participant.
2. **Run preparation inside `asyncio.TaskGroup`.** An ordinary child failure causes unfinished siblings to be cancelled, and leaving the task-group context waits for its tasks to finish. Handle the resulting failure outside that context. Multiple failures may be reported together in an `ExceptionGroup`, a container for separate exceptions.
3. **On preparation failure, release every remaining uncommitted ledger entry and wait for those releases to finish before sending the failure response.** Include reservations belonging to tasks that finished preparation successfully before another task failed. Cancelling unfinished tasks alone cannot release those. Give releases one owner, or make them safe to repeat without refunding twice.
4. **If all preparations succeed, perform the toy model's group commit in one coordinator step with no suspension between its state changes.** Validate what is needed first, move the prepared quantities to `committed`, and mark their ledger entries committed. Under the stated one-loop, in-memory assumptions, other tasks cannot interleave through an `await` within that step.
5. **Publish participant success only after the coordinator has established group success.** On a later failure, release only entries established to be uncommitted; never treat every exception as proof that a committed effect can be reversed.

The ledger is essential even with `TaskGroup`: that API controls task lifetimes, not ownership of resources already acquired. Likewise, if releasing a real reservation can fail or cleanup can be interrupted, the coordinator needs supervised cleanup that survives request cancellation, records failed releases, and establishes completion. It cannot truthfully claim the “released before response” requirement has been met merely because it attempted release. [A01-NOTES]

## Where an external commit changes the problem

The non-suspending counter update is not a model of an atomic external transfer. A remote service can complete a transfer and then lose the acknowledgement; cancellation or a timeout can leave the local process uncertain whether it committed. Multiple transfers can also succeed only partly before another fails.

That requires additional design: durable records of each transfer's state, stable identifiers that prevent a retry from creating a second transfer, a way to query or reconcile an uncertain outcome, and a recovery owner that outlives the request. Reservation release must distinguish known uncommitted work from known committed or unresolved work. An unresolved outcome cannot safely be treated as an uncommitted reservation.

If the provider offers a genuine atomic group operation or a prepare/commit protocol, the coordinator can use its documented guarantees. Otherwise, the stated rules permit a failed group with an already committed transfer that must remain committed. The response and retry policy must represent that partial or pending outcome without publishing participant success or pretending all effects were undone. If the business additionally requires that no transfer ever remain committed after any group failure, it needs stronger external transaction support or a changed business protocol; switching from `gather` to `TaskGroup` does not supply that guarantee.
