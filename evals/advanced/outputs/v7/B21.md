The canceled accounts were five Starter, one Pro, and one Enterprise: **7 accounts** carrying **5 × 20 + 100 + 1,000 = 1,200 units per month** at opening. The four surviving Enterprise accounts expanded from 4,000 to 6,000, adding **2,000 units per month**. [B21-S1; B21-S2]

The totals reconcile in two useful ways:

- Accounts: **65 − 7 + 22 = 80**.
- Recurring revenue: **7,000 − 1,200 + 2,000 + 600 = 8,400 units per month**.

The Enterprise expansion comes from existing account identifiers; it does not create new customers. [B21-S1–S3]

## Use a separate label for each question

Using the supplied definitions, with percentages rounded to two decimal places:

| Metric | Calculation | What it tells the manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account retention** | 58 ÷ 65 = **89.23%** | What share of opening customers remained |
| **Net recurring-revenue retention** | 7,800 ÷ 7,000 = **111.43%** | How opening-cohort recurring revenue changed, including expansion |
| **Gross recurring-revenue retention** | 5,800 ÷ 7,000 = **82.86%** | How much opening recurring revenue remained before counting expansion |

For gross retention, cap each survivor's contribution at its opening amount: **45 × 20 + 9 × 100 + 4 × 1,000 = 5,800**. Equivalently, opening revenue of 7,000 less canceled revenue of 1,200 leaves 5,800; no surviving account contracted. [B21-S1; B21-S2; B21-S4]

Net retention above 100% therefore does not mean every customer stayed. The cohort lost seven customers and 1,200 units of their monthly contracts, while four large customers added 2,000 units. Revenue weights accounts by their spending; account retention counts each account once.

The slide using **8,400 ÷ 7,000 = 120%** combines retention, expansion, and acquisition. An honest label is **“closing total monthly recurring revenue as a percentage of opening revenue,”** or, more directly, **“total monthly recurring revenue growth: 20%.”** It is not cohort retention because its numerator includes 600 units from newly acquired customers. [B21-S3; B21-S4]

## What the snapshot cannot establish

**Cash collected:** Annual prepayments and collections of overdue invoices concern different service periods. Their amounts are missing, so neither cash collected nor its change can be calculated from these contract snapshots. Use payment records matched to invoices. [B21-S3]

**Earned revenue:** Closing monthly contract amounts do not show how much service was provided at each price during the month. Upgrade and cancellation dates, service periods, and the corresponding revenue ledger are needed. [B21-S2]

**Profit:** Higher recurring revenue does not establish higher profit. Support workload increased, but the packet supplies no cost amounts or complete earned-revenue ledger. Compare revenue earned with the costs attributable to the same period. [B21-S2; B21-S3]

**Cancellation reasons and satisfaction:** The survey excludes canceled accounts, and its response rate is unknown. Praise from respondents describes those respondents; it does not explain why others canceled or establish satisfaction across all customers. Seven cancellations occurred, and the snapshot cannot establish that cancellations have now stopped. Seek evidence from departing customers, report survey coverage and response rates, and distinguish reported reasons from demonstrated causes. [B21-S2–S4]

**Future durability:** One month's expansion does not establish that large accounts will maintain their spending or that customer losses will stop. Track successive cohorts, account-level expansion and cancellation, and performance over several periods before making a durability claim. [B21-S1–S3]
