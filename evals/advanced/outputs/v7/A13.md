### What can be calculated

In the hypothetical experiment, all twenty null hypotheses are true and each independent test has probability 0.05 of producing `p < 0.05`. The probability that none does so is `0.95²⁰`. Therefore,

`P(at least one false positive) = 1 − 0.95²⁰ ≈ 0.641514 = 64.15%.`

This is a probability about repeated experiments under the supplied assumptions, not the probability that this study's selected claim is false. [A13-PLAN]

To control the probability of any false rejection within a twenty-outcome family at 0.05, Bonferroni uses the per-test threshold:

`0.05/20 = 0.0025.`

### What the p-value and effect estimate mean

For a prespecified, correctly calibrated test, `p = 0.018` means that **if the null hypothesis and test assumptions held**, results at least as extreme as the observed result would occur with probability 1.8%. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true, that the feature has no effect, or that this particular conclusion is a false positive. ### A concrete confirmatory follow-up

Run a new randomized experiment assigning users to the feature or a concurrent control. Before examining its results:

1. **Specify one primary outcome:** mean saved clicks per eligible session over a fixed follow-up window, with an exact definition of a “saved click” and session eligibility. Account for repeated sessions from the same user in the analysis.
2. **Set a meaningful minimum benefit:** choose a threshold, `δ` clicks per session, that would justify the feature. Determine a fixed sample size using an independent estimate of variability, the target benefit, and desired power—the probability of detecting that target benefit. The present packet cannot supply a numerical sample-size requirement.
3. **Fix the stopping rule:** complete that sample and follow-up window, then perform one final primary analysis. Do not extend enrollment because the p-value is disappointing. If interim decisions are essential, specify a sequential procedure beforehand that controls error across those decisions.
4. **Fix the decision criterion:** report the effect and a valid 95% confidence interval. For a positive statistical finding, require the interval to exclude zero in the favorable direction. For a claim of a worthwhile benefit, require its lower bound to exceed the preselected `δ`. Prespecify any additional confirmatory outcomes and their multiple-testing correction.

Also measure actual task completion time and relevant adverse effects under a prespecified plan. The original study did not establish that more nominally saved clicks translate into less effort.
