**An intervention can repair an intermediate part of a system while leaving another requirement for the final outcome unmet.** In these tanks, adding grazers restored low film and high light, but M remained low and colony establishment remained low. Restoring both grazers and M produced a much larger colony mean. That pattern supports the proposed explanation involving two requirements; it does not prove every link in the network. [B15-S1–S3]

## What each intervention changed

The randomized experiment assigned four tanks to each condition. These are reported condition means, with measurements in the packet’s arbitrary units. [B15-S2]

| Condition | Grazers | Film | Light | M | Established colonies |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Untreated | 10 | 1 | 80 | 8 | 40 |
| Warmed | 3 | 7 | 20 | 2 | 5 |
| Warmed + grazers | 10 | 1 | 80 | 2 | 6 |
| Warmed + M | 3 | 7 | 20 | 8 | 7 |
| Warmed + both | 10 | 1 | 80 | 8 | 38 |

In the proposed model, warming can damage two routes to colony establishment: it may reduce shelter and thereby grazers, increasing film and reducing light; it may also reduce M through a separate route. Colonies require both sufficient light and sufficient M. [B15-S1]

Adding grazers bypasses the shelter step: animals are placed directly into the tanks. Film falls from 7 to 1 and light rises from 20 to 80, so calling this intervention a complete failure would discard its substantial intermediate effects. But M stays at 2. If M remains limiting, improving light need not restore colony establishment. [B15-S2–S3]

Conversely, adding M raises its measured concentration from 2 to 8, but film remains high and light remains low. That single intervention leaves the other proposed requirement unmet. With both additions, the measured light and M values match those in untreated tanks, and the colony mean reaches 38 rather than 5. These findings are consistent with the two requirements jointly constraining establishment. They do not establish exact thresholds or prove that the treated and untreated conditions are equivalent. [B15-S1–S3]

## Why the combined result is not impossible

The two single additions increase the reported colony mean by 1 and 2 relative to warmed tanks. Simply adding those improvements predicts:

**5 + (6 − 5) + (7 − 5) = 8 colonies.**

That calculation assumes each intervention has the same effect whether the other intervention is present. The observed means do not follow that assumption:

- With M low, adding grazers changes the mean from 5 to 6: **+1**.
- With M restored, adding grazers changes the mean from 7 to 38: **+31**.

The difference between those improvements is **30 colonies**. This is an interaction: the effect of one intervention depends on whether the other is present. A large interaction is plausible when either unmet requirement can prevent establishment. Meeting one requirement makes satisfying the other much more consequential. These calculations describe the reported means; without individual results or uncertainty estimates, they do not establish the precision of that interaction. [B15-S2]

## What the experiment supports—and what it leaves open

**The randomized comparisons directly test the effects of the treatment packages.** In the reported means, warming changes the measured conditions and establishment; grazer addition changes film and light; and the joint additions produce much higher establishment. However, variation among individual tanks is unavailable, so the strength and precision of these estimated effects cannot be assessed from the means alone. The small single-rescue differences also cannot be confidently treated as either zero or reliably positive. [B15-S2]

**Not every arrow between measurements was independently tested.** Film and light change together after grazer addition. That supports the proposed sequence, but light was not manipulated separately to establish that the change in light caused the colony response. The film of one grazer eating shows that consumption can occur, not how much of the tank-level film reduction it explains. [B15-S3]

**The frond explanation is bypassed, not proved.** Added grazers do not restore fronds, and the reported experiment gives no frond measurements. The shelter pilot is consistent with shelter increasing grazers, but shelter also caused shade and colony establishment was not measured. The dual rescue therefore cannot prove that frond decline was warming’s only damaging effect. [B15-S1–S3]

**Measured M may not capture everything the addition did.** M addition could change unmeasured conditions that affect colonies. Similarly, finding lower M after warming does not by itself isolate a direct warming-to-M path from every possible intermediary. The results leave the two-path model plausible, while alternative mechanisms remain possible. [B15-S1–S3]

## Two focused follow-up comparisons

1. **Control light independently and cross that comparison with M restoration.** Within warmed tanks, hold grazers, film, and shelter constant while setting light at the colony surface to low or high levels; compare each light level with and without M restoration, using matched handling controls. Measure actual light, M, and colony establishment. This tests whether changing light itself affects establishment and whether its benefit depends on M, without relying on grazers to change light. Because M addition may have other effects, this would still estimate the effect of that addition unless its specificity is separately established.

2. **Compare artificial shelter with a structure that casts equivalent shade but provides no usable shelter.** Keep M adequate in both conditions and measure grazers, film, light, and establishment. This addresses the pilot’s shading problem and tests whether shelter changes the grazer pathway when the proposed mineral constraint is relieved. It would support or weaken the shelter mechanism; it would not alone establish that warming damaged natural fronds.

For both comparisons, retain individual tank outcomes and report uncertainty. That would distinguish the proposed mechanisms more clearly while also showing how reliably the observed differences can be estimated.
