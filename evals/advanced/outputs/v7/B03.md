## Which documents control?

The April 2 enacted ordinance sets the scope and eligibility rules. The May 10 implementing circular supplies declaration requirements and a filing date within the office’s authority. It cannot change the ordinance’s agreement or delivery cutoffs. The May 11 slide is an informal aid, and its shorthand omits a required condition and substitutes the undefined phrase “orders received” for a signed purchase agreement. [B03-S1–S2]

The May 28 amendment is a proposal requiring a separate council vote. The June 3 digest’s “June extension” label does not establish enactment, because it links only to that draft. The packet contains no record establishing that an amendment became effective; that is different from proof that no vote ever occurred. Until an enacted, applicable amendment is established, the supplied enacted ordinance is the basis for these decisions. The modernization brochure contributes no eligibility evidence. [B03-S3]

## The dates answer different questions

| Date or event | Its role under the supplied rules |
| --- | --- |
| April 2 publication | Identifies the enacted ordinance; April 2 is also the agreement cutoff specified by that ordinance |
| Signed purchase agreement | Must predate April 2 to qualify for transition treatment |
| Physical delivery to the customer | Must occur before June 1 for transition treatment; dispatch, customs events, and intermediate storage do not substitute |
| First placement into service | From June 1, commercial equipment first placed into service within Port Meridian requires a certificate unless it qualifies for the transition route |
| Declaration filing | Permitted until July 15, including after first use when substantive transition conditions were already met |
| June 12 consultation close | A procedural date for the proposal, not an enacted delivery extension |

Thus July 15 is a filing date, not extra time to purchase or deliver otherwise ineligible equipment. [B03-S1–S3]

## Birch: eligible conditions, with filing still to complete

Birch’s April 1 signed agreement predates April 2, and its May 31 physical delivery to its Port Meridian commercial customer predates June 1. Both substantive transition conditions are met. Its June 2 first use does not defeat eligibility: the circular expressly allows filing after first use. [B03-S1–S2, B03-S4]

The July 10 appointment is before July 15, so Birch can use that appointment to file a declaration with the signed agreement, proof of customer delivery, and serial number. An appointment is not evidence that filing has occurred. If the complete declaration is filed within the permitted period, Birch may use the transition route instead of a certificate. This also exposes the slide’s misleading wording: “before April” would appear to exclude an April 1 agreement that the ordinance actually permits.

## Cedar: two separate facts remain unresolved

**First, where was Cedar first placed into service?** Its June 4 first operation was in the leased workshop, but the workshop’s district assignment is missing. A warehouse postal address saying “Meridian Bay” does not establish that assignment. If the workshop is outside Port Meridian, this ordinance does not impose its certificate requirement; the packet cannot determine any requirement under the other district’s regime. If the workshop is inside Port Meridian, Cedar falls within the ordinance’s commercial-use and service-date scope. [B03-S1, B03-S4]

**Second, did physical customer delivery occur before June 1?** Cedar’s March 20 agreement qualifies, but “delivered May 25 to a warehouse” does not establish whether that was delivery to the customer or intermediate storage. If it was physical customer delivery, the delivery condition is met, and—if the workshop is inside Port Meridian—Cedar can use a properly completed transition declaration filed by July 15. The same conclusion follows if some other proven customer delivery occurred before June 1. If customer delivery occurred on or after June 1, Cedar cannot use the enacted transition exception and needs a certificate for first service inside the district. If the delivery event remains unproved, transition eligibility remains unresolved. [B03-S1–S2, B03-S4]

The team should therefore determine the governing enacted text, check where first service occurred, test the agreement and customer-delivery conditions separately, and then complete the appropriate certificate or declaration process. Publication dates help identify documents; they do not replace these checks.
