**J1 executes two failed attempts and expires at time 6 without starting its third attempt.** J2 succeeds on its second attempt, and J3 succeeds on its first. The three-attempt setting is an upper limit; J1's deadline prevents the remaining attempt from running. [A22-RETRY; A22-DEADLINE]

## Actual worker timeline

All times below are seconds from time zero. An interval such as `0–1` means the attempt starts at 0 and completes at 1.

| Time or worker interval | Selected work and scheduled eligibility | Outcome and scheduling consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 | J1, attempt 1; eligible at 0 | Fails at 1. Schedule attempt 2 for `1 + 1 = 2`. |
| 1–2 | J2, attempt 1; eligible at 0 | Fails at 2. Schedule attempt 2 for `2 + 1 = 3`. |
| 2–3 | J3, attempt 1; eligible at 0 | Succeeds at 3; job ends. |
| 3–4 | J1, attempt 2; eligible at 2 | Fails at 4. Schedule attempt 3 for `4 + 2 = 6`. |
| 4–5 | J2, attempt 2; eligible at 3 | Succeeds at 5; job ends. |
| 5–6 | No attempt is eligible | Worker is idle until J1's scheduled time, 6. |
| At 6 | Select J1, attempt 3; eligible at 6 | It would complete at 7, exceeding deadline 6. Expire J1 immediately; the attempt never starts. |

This follows the queue ordering and deadline rules directly. Five attempts execute, consuming five seconds of worker time, with one idle second before the final expiration. [A22-SCHEDULER; A22-RETRY; A22-DEADLINE; A22-INPUT]

Initially, all scheduled times are zero, so lexical identifiers put J1 before J2 before J3. At time 2, J3's original attempt still has scheduled time 0, while J1's retry has scheduled time 2. J3 therefore goes first. At time 3, J1's retry, scheduled for 2, precedes J2's retry, scheduled for 3.

J1's third attempt is enqueued at time 4 even though its eligibility at 6 makes an on-time completion impossible. The specification deliberately checks the deadline only upon selection, so expiration occurs at 6, not at enqueue time. An attempt starting at 5 and completing exactly at 6 would pass the deadline check, but J1's retry is not eligible at 5. [A22-DEADLINE]

## Which listed outcomes happen

| Job | Outcomes actually realized | Final state | Unexecuted listed outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | Attempt 1: failure; attempt 2: failure | Expired at 6 | Attempt 3's success remains hypothetical |
| J2 | Attempt 1: failure; attempt 2: success | Succeeded at 5 | None |
| J3 | Attempt 1: success | Succeeded at 3 | None |

The input specifies what would happen **if an attempt executes**. It does not override eligibility or deadlines. Expiration is not a third executed failure, and J1's unused attempt allowance is not evidence of success. [A22-INPUT]

## Evaluating the incident claims

1. **“J1 retries at absolute times 1 and 2” is incorrect.** Those are the first and second backoff delays, measured from the corresponding failed attempt's completion. J1's retries become eligible at `1 + 1 = 2` and `4 + 2 = 6`. Eligibility also does not guarantee immediate execution: its first retry waits until time 3.
2. **“J1's retry must precede J3” is incorrect.** The queue prioritizes scheduled eligibility time, then job identifier, then attempt number. It does not give retries a special privilege over older eligible work. J3's scheduled time 0 precedes J1's retry time 2.
3. **“Maximum attempts guarantees three tries and eventual success” is incorrect.** Three includes the initial attempt and caps executed attempts. A success can stop a job earlier, and an absolute deadline can prevent a remaining attempt from starting. J1 reaches its deadline with only two attempts executed.

Maximum attempts limits repeated work; the absolute deadline limits how late a useful result may complete. Neither replaces the other. Removing the deadline check would change the contract and allow J1 to run from 6 to 7, producing the listed success after its permitted completion time. A more attractive graph is not a justification for that policy change. [A22-CLAIMS; A22-DEADLINE]

## Production handling and instrumentation

A sensible policy records a distinct terminal **expired** state, the deadline, two executed attempts, and the reason the next attempt was refused. Surface that state to the caller or owning workflow. Depending on business needs, route expired work to a separate review queue or allow deliberate resubmission with a newly authorized deadline. Whether late work remains valuable should determine such a policy; do not silently discard the deadline.

Record, for each attempt, its job and attempt identifiers, enqueue time, scheduled eligibility time, selection time, actual start and completion times if executed, outcome, and deadline decision. Also record the retry delay selected and the job's terminal reason. These timestamps separate:

- **Retry delay:** from the previous failed completion to the retry's scheduled eligibility. J1's first delay is 1–2.
- **Queue waiting after eligibility:** from eligibility to actual start. That retry waits from 2–3 because the worker is occupied.
- **Execution time:** from actual start to completion. It runs from 3–4.
- **Expiration without execution:** J1's third attempt is selected at 6 but has no actual start or completion timestamp.

Tracking worker idle intervals also shows that 5–6 is a wait for eligibility, not time spent executing J1.

In a larger deployment, random variation in retry delays—jitter—can spread out retries when many jobs on different workers fail together, preventing their synchronized retries from producing another load spike. This exercise explicitly has no jitter, so it does not alter the deterministic timeline above. [A22-RETRY]
