**The certified totals establish that between 100 and 500 learners have both flags. They do not establish whether more than 300 do.** Overlap is unavoidable, but a definite conclusion that the formal opening trigger is met—or is not met—is unsupported. [B30-S1, B30-S2]

## Derive the possible overlap

Let (x) be the number with both flags. Adding 600 and 500 counts each learner with both twice. The number with at least one flag is therefore

\[
600+500-x=1{,}100-x.
\]

That number cannot exceed the population of 1,000, so (x\ge100). The overlap also cannot exceed either flag's total, so (x\le\min(600,500)=500). Thus

\[
\boxed{100\le x\le500.}
\]

The sum of 1,100 flag memberships is not an inconsistency: memberships and distinct learners are different counts. These bounds follow from the certified totals alone, without assuming independence or any particular association. [B30-S1]

More generally, the four groups would contain:

- Both flags: (x).
- A only: (600-x).
- B only: (500-x).
- Neither: (x-100).

Every integer (x) from 100 through 500 makes these counts nonnegative, preserves both certified flag totals, and sums to 1,000. That also shows the bounds are attainable. [B30-S1]

## Two feasible populations give opposite decisions

| Population composition | Minimum overlap | Maximum overlap |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Both flags | 100 | 500 |
| A only | 500 | 100 |
| B only | 400 | 0 |
| Neither | 0 | 400 |
| **Total learners** | **1,000** | **1,000** |
| Total with A | 600 | 600 |
| Total with B | 500 | 500 |
| **More than 300 with both?** | **No** | **Yes** |

Both compositions satisfy all certified population counts. One does not meet the trigger; the other does. Some unavoidable overlap therefore cannot establish that the center must open. Conversely, missing identifiers do not prevent useful mathematical deductions. [B30-S1, B30-S2]

The threshold is strict: **300 does not qualify; 301 does.** The later informal summary neither amends the rule nor clearly identifies the relevant group, so it cannot replace “more than 300 with both flags.” [B30-S2]

## Why estimates and supplementary material do not settle it

Multiplying (0.6\times0.5\times1{,}000=300) adds an independence assumption. The marginal totals do not supply that assumption. A simulation assigning flags independently reproduces the consequences of its model; ten thousand runs do not reveal the actual population's missing association. Even simulations constrained to preserve the two exact totals must still choose how flag memberships are paired. The two feasible populations above show why the observed totals alone cannot make that choice. [B30-S1, B30-S3]

The voluntary group's 80 reports out of 100 respondents do not provide a random validation sample. Recruitment targeted people seeking intensive support, flags were unverified, and linked identifiers were unavailable. Its proportion cannot be assumed to describe the full population or resolve the certified overlap. Overall satisfaction measures something else entirely. [B30-S4]

## The minimum additional information

For this particular decision, the minimum sufficient release is a **verified aggregate yes/no answer to “Do at least 301 of these 1,000 learners have both flags on the specified date?”** An exact overlap count would also settle it, but discloses more than the decision requires. A certified bound entirely above 300 or entirely at or below 300 would likewise suffice. [B30-S1, B30-S2]

An authorized custodian can calculate the threshold result within the existing data controls. The administrator does not need names, exported linked identifiers, individual flag histories, or a complete cross-tabulation merely to apply this trigger. The operational need is to determine which side of 300 the overlap occupies, not to collect unnecessary personal details.
