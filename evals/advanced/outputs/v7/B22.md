The museum should treat **mechanical failure as an unresolved explanation**, not an established fact. The strongest contemporary evidence is narrower: the diarist reported hearing no northern answer at the south quay. The packet does not securely establish that the North Bell never rang, still less that its mechanism could not ring. [B22-S1]

## Three claims with different evidential demands

1. **“The diarist did not hear it.”** This concerns one listener at one location and time. It is what the diary directly reports.
2. **“The bell did not ring.”** This concerns the bell itself. It requires ruling out ringing that the diarist could not hear.
3. **“The bell was unable to ring.”** This adds a mechanical explanation. Even an actually silent bell might have been operable but not rung. [B22-S1; B22-S3]

Hearing the South Bell shows that the diarist heard some warning sounds, but does not establish reliable audibility of the North Bell. A warehouse ridge lay between it and the quay, and normal audibility and the wind's effect are unspecified. [B22-S1]

## Agreement is not necessarily independent confirmation

The chronicle was written eighteen years later and lists the diary as a source. It may therefore inherit the diary's account, while strengthening “no answer reached us” into a statement about the bell's silence. The packet does not establish that this particular sentence came from the diary, so dependence is plausible rather than fully traced. [B22-S2]

Dependence of the school history on the chronicle is more strongly indicated: its bibliography names the chronicle, and it repeats both an unusual phrase and a mistaken gate spelling. Counting these as three independent witnesses would give potentially inherited information three times the weight. [B22-S2]

That does not make the later books useless. They show how later writers presented the storm and connected the bell's silence to absent leadership. They are evidence about historical storytelling and the development of a public-memory narrative, even where their independent value for the storm-night mechanism is uncertain. Their publication dates alone neither disqualify them nor make their event claims reliable. [B22-S2]

## Explanations still compatible with the evidence

**The bell rang but was not heard at the quay.** The diary's location-specific wording allows this. Strong wind is independently documented, the ridge could obstruct sound, and the reconstruction shows that a ringing bell can sometimes be inaudible there in strong wind. But the reconstruction differs in height and material: it establishes a plausible alternative, not that this alternative actually occurred or how probable it was in year 27. [B22-S1; B22-S3]

**The bell did not ring because a mechanical problem prevented it.** This fits the later descriptions of silence. A rope replacement two months later is compatible with an earlier rope failure, but does not date that failure to the storm or establish any failure at all: replacement could have been preventive. The possibly dependent texts cannot turn that invoice into a storm-night diagnosis. [B22-S2; B22-S3]

**The bell was operable but was not rung.** Silence would also fit the listening report without a mechanical fault. The packet supplies no operator record to establish or exclude this possibility. The later leadership symbolism is an interpretation, not direct evidence that an operator failed to act. [B22-S1; B22-S2]

The weather log strengthens confidence that the storm and flooding occurred; it does not decide among these explanations. Festival attendance has no bearing on the original mechanism. [B22-S3; B22-S4]

## What would change the conclusion

An authenticated storm-night account from someone beside the North Bell, independent of the diary, would be particularly useful. A report of hearing or operating it would support the inaudibility explanation. An account of attempting to ring it and finding a broken rope, or a dated inspection identifying a fault before that attempt, would support mechanical incapacity. An operator's record of leaving a working bell unrung would support the third explanation. The existing later replacement invoice lacks those decisive details.

There is no justified basis for the proposed 97% probability: witness reliability, source dependence, and the relative fit of the evidence to each explanation have not been established. A suitable museum label would be:

> A diary from the storm night reports that no answer from the North Bell was heard at the south quay. Later accounts describe the bell as silent, but may draw on earlier accounts. Whether it rang, and whether a mechanical fault prevented it from ringing, remains uncertain.

That wording preserves both the contemporary evidence and the alternatives it leaves open. [B22-S1–S4]
