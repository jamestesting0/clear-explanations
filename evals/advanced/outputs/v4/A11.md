The fitted model associates acceleration with a lower **median duration** throughout the observed 100–200 MiB range. It does **not** predict a lower arithmetic mean throughout that range, and this observational study does not establish what switching the feature on would cause.

## Median durations and size effects

Let \(\mu\) denote the fitted mean of \(\ln(T)\). Substituting the mode indicator gives

\[
\mu_{\text{standard}}=4.00+0.20x,
\qquad
\mu_{\text{accelerated}}=3.90+0.05x.
\]

Under the supplied normal residual distributions, exponentiating these values gives conditional **medians** in seconds. The accelerated-to-standard median ratio at a given size is

\[
R_{\text{median}}(x)
=\exp(\mu_{\text{accelerated}}-\mu_{\text{standard}})
=\exp(-0.10-0.15x).
\]

| File size | Standard median | Accelerated median | Accelerated / standard | Median duration change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 MiB, \(x=0\) | 54.60 s | 49.40 s | 0.9048 | 9.52% lower |
| 200 MiB, \(x=1\) | 66.69 s | 51.94 s | 0.7788 | 22.12% lower |

All durations above are seconds: for example, 54.60 seconds is approximately 54,600 milliseconds. The differences between the fitted medians are about 5.20 seconds and 14.75 seconds, respectively; neither is a guaranteed saving for an individual request. [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY]

The coefficient \(-0.10\) is a difference in fitted log duration **at 100 MiB**, where \(x=0\). Its median interpretation is multiplication by \(e^{-0.10}\), not subtraction of 0.10 seconds.

Within each mode, adding 100 MiB increases \(x\) by one:

- **Standard:** the log-duration slope is 0.20, so the median multiplies by \(e^{0.20}=1.2214\), an increase of 22.14%.
- **Accelerated:** the slope is \(0.20-0.15=0.05\), so the median multiplies by \(e^{0.05}=1.0513\), an increase of 5.13%.

For a one-MiB increase within the observed range, the corresponding median increases are approximately 0.2002% and 0.0500%.

The interaction \(-0.15xz\) makes the size slope smaller in accelerated mode. Equivalently, each additional 100 MiB multiplies the accelerated-to-standard median ratio by \(e^{-0.15}=0.8607\), reducing that ratio by 13.93%. It is neither a 15% saving per MiB nor a fixed percentage-point change. [A11-MODEL]

## Arithmetic means and batch consumption

The arithmetic mean requires the supplied variance correction:

\[
E[T\mid x,z]=\exp(\mu+\sigma_z^2/2).
\]

The standard-mode correction is \(0.40^2/2=0.08\); the accelerated-mode correction is \(0.80^2/2=0.32\). Therefore,

\[
R_{\text{mean}}(x)
=\exp\!\left[-0.10-0.15x+(0.32-0.08)\right]
=\exp(0.14-0.15x).
\]

| File size | Standard arithmetic mean | Accelerated arithmetic mean | Accelerated / standard | Mean duration change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 MiB | 59.15 s | 68.03 s | \(e^{0.14}=1.1503\) | 15.03% higher |
| 200 MiB | 72.24 s | 71.52 s | \(e^{-0.01}=0.9900\) | 1.00% lower |

Accelerated mode has greater spread in log duration. After exponentiation, unusually long durations pull its arithmetic mean upward more strongly, even though its median is lower. Thus, at 100 MiB, a lower typical duration coexists with a higher average duration. The fitted means are equal at approximately 193.33 MiB; acceleration has the higher mean below that size and the lower mean above it within the observed range. [A11-MODEL] [A11-IDENTITY]

The variance correction converts a distribution's log-scale parameters into its arithmetic mean; it is not another regression coefficient or an extra term to add to an individual observation's log duration. Because each mode's variance is constant across size, its arithmetic mean has the same multiplicative size slope as its median. For a batch, expected summed durations come from summing the appropriate arithmetic means, so the batch's size mix matters.

## What the evidence supports

Mode selection was voluntary. Graphics-card quality, model complexity, and prior retries could influence both that selection and duration, and adjustment for file size alone does not remove those differences. The fitted comparisons describe associations between requests at the same file size; they do not establish a causal benefit from switching the feature on. [A11-STUDY]

A randomized experiment assigning eligible requests to standard or accelerated mode would address that question more directly. Balance or stratify assignment by file size and relevant hardware, and prespecify both median duration and arithmetic mean duration as outcomes. Controlled repeat runs of the same workloads with randomized mode order could also help separate mode effects from workload and hardware differences.
