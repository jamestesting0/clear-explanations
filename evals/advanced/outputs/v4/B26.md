Under the stipulated accounting, total social surplus is **900 in the baseline, 700 under Policy Tax, and 920 under Policy Clean**. Tax revenue and sales revenue describe money changing hands; social surplus measures the purchasers' benefits remaining after real production, nuisance, and administration costs.

## Distribution of benefits and costs

Buyer surplus is the value buyers receive minus what they pay. Producer contribution before common fixed costs is gross sales minus tax remitted and production resource costs. Council net receipts subtract administration or monitoring costs from tax receipts. Nuisance damage is a separate loss borne by affected residents.

All figures below are currency units for the whole period. Each scenario produces 100 units, with total purchaser benefit of \(100\times22=2{,}200\) (B26-S1–S3).

| Item | Baseline | Policy Tax | Policy Clean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Total purchaser benefit | 2,200 | 2,200 | 2,200 |
| Buyer payments / producer gross sales | 1,500 | 2,000 | 1,700 |
| **Buyer surplus** | **700** | **200** | **500** |
| Production resource costs | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,200 |
| Tax remitted to council | 0 | 500 | 0 |
| **Producer contribution before common fixed costs** | **500** | **500** | **500** |
| Administration or monitoring costs | 0 | 200 | 80 |
| **Council net receipts / costs** | **0** | **300** | **−80** |
| Nuisance damage | 300 | 300 | 0 |
| **Total social surplus** | **900** | **700** | **920** |

Summing the parties' positions gives:

- Baseline: \(700+500+0-300=900\).
- Tax: \(200+500+300-300=700\).
- Clean: \(500+500-80-0=920\).

The council's net position already includes administration or monitoring, so those costs must not be subtracted again in this calculation.

## Why transfers cancel

Let buyer payments be \(P\), tax receipts \(T\), production costs \(C\), administration costs \(A\), purchaser benefit \(V\), and nuisance damage \(D\). Adding the parties' positions gives

\[
(V-P)+(P-T-C)+(T-A)-D=V-C-A-D.
\]

Payments leave one island party and reach another, so \(P\) and \(T\) cancel. This reconciles the distributional calculation with the direct resource calculation:

- Baseline: \(2{,}200-1{,}000-300=900\).
- Tax: \(2{,}200-1{,}000-300-200=700\).
- Clean: \(2{,}200-1{,}200-0-80=920\).

The retained tax receipts are not an additional 300 of social benefit on top of this accounting. Any benefit from future spending would need separate evidence, which the packet does not provide.

## What the comparisons establish

**Tax reduces surplus by 200 relative to baseline.** Its only stipulated change in real costs is administration. Buyers lose 500 of surplus; producers' additional 500 of gross sales is entirely remitted as tax, leaving their contribution unchanged. The council retains 300 after administration. Nuisance damage stays at 300 because neither output nor the production method changes. This scenario therefore supplies no nuisance reduction to offset administration costs.

**Clean increases surplus by 20 relative to baseline.** It avoids 300 of nuisance damage while using 200 more production resources and 80 for monitoring: \(300-200-80=20\). Buyers' additional expenditure of 200 does not by itself establish a social loss. Their surplus falls, but affected residents avoid damage. Clean exceeds Tax by 220 in total surplus.

These rankings use the exercise's equal weighting: one currency unit of benefit to one resident offsets one currency unit of loss to another. That is a value judgment, not a demonstration that every distribution is acceptable. A policy can raise the total while making particular people worse off, without compensating them.

The stipulated outcomes establish neither an optimal future tax rate nor how another rate would change prices, output, production methods, or damage. Those questions require behavioral and cost evidence beyond these three scenarios. Producer contribution also remains a measure before the omitted common fixed costs, not a complete calculation of personal income or final profit.
