Under the stated calibration and hold model, the analog total is **180,000 joules = 50 watt-hours = 0.050 kWh**, and the time-weighted mean power is **1,500 watts**. The register's displayed increase is 0.052 kWh, but its conservative possible range includes 0.050 kWh, so the packet does not establish an inconsistency.

## Calibrated powers and interval energies

Apply the calibration \(P=2V-200\), where \(V\) is the numerical reading in millivolts and \(P\) is power in watts:

| Elapsed time | Reading | Calibrated power |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 s | 600 mV | 1,000 W |
| 30 s | 1,100 mV | 2,000 W |
| 90 s | 600 mV | 1,000 W |
| 120 s | 100 mV | 0 W |

The −200 W offset is part of every calibrated power calculation. It is not an error allowance. [A14-CALIBRATION]

The acquisition contract holds each sampled power constant until the next timestamp. Thus each interval's energy is its held power multiplied by its duration:

| Interval | Duration | Held power | Energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| [0, 30) s | 30 s | 1,000 W | 30,000 J |
| [30, 90) s | 60 s | 2,000 W | 120,000 J |
| [90, 120) s | 30 s | 1,000 W | 30,000 J |
| **Total** | **120 s** | — | **180,000 J** |

The 0 W reading at 120 s starts a new level at the boundary. It contributes no energy to **[0,120)** because it lasts for zero seconds inside that interval. [A14-SAMPLES]

Using the supplied unit definitions,

\[
E=180{,}000\ \text{J}
=\frac{180{,}000}{3600}\ \text{Wh}
=50\ \text{Wh}
=0.050\ \text{kWh}.
\]

The time-weighted mean power is

\[
\bar P_{\text{time}}
=\frac{E}{120\ \text{s}}
=\frac{1000(30)+2000(60)+1000(30)}{120}
=1500\ \text{W}.
\]

## Why both spreadsheet versions are wrong

The unweighted mean of all four powers is

\[
\frac{1000+2000+1000+0}{4}=1000\ \text{W}.
\]

That gives every reading 25% of the weight. The correct time weights are **25%, 50%, 25%, and 0%**: the 2,000 W level lasts twice as long as either 1,000 W level, and the endpoint level has no duration within the target interval. Merely dropping the endpoint would still leave an incorrect unweighted average of unequally long intervals. [A14-CALIBRATION] [A14-SAMPLES]

The author's calculation gives

\[
1000\times120/1000=120.
\]

But watts times seconds gives **joules**, and dividing joules by 1,000 gives **kilojoules**, not kilowatt-hours. The displayed “120 kWh” therefore has both a weighting error and a unit error. [A14-SPREADSHEET]

Correcting only the divisor to \(3600\times1000\) produces

\[
\frac{1000\times120}{3{,}600{,}000}
=0.033333\ldots\ \text{kWh}.
\]

Its units are now correct, but it retains the wrong 1,000 W mean. It understates the model's energy by one-third.

## What trapezoidal integration assumes

Trapezoidal integration uses the average of each interval's two endpoint powers. It therefore corresponds to power changing **linearly** between samples:

| Interval | Trapezoidal energy |
|---|---:|
| [0, 30) s | \((1000+2000)\times30/2=45{,}000\) J |
| [30, 90) s | \((2000+1000)\times60/2=90{,}000\) J |
| [90, 120) s | \((1000+0)\times30/2=15{,}000\) J |

The total would be **150,000 J = 41.6667 Wh ≈ 0.0416667 kWh**, corresponding to a mean power of 1,250 W.

That calculation treats the final interval as a gradual decline from 1,000 W to 0 W. The supplied contract instead holds power at 1,000 W until 120 s, then changes it to 0 W. An endpoint can influence a trapezoid through the assumed linear path without itself occupying positive duration. Here that path contradicts the stated model. Rectangles implement the given hold contract exactly; trapezoids are not automatically more accurate. [A14-CALIBRATION] [A14-SPREADSHEET]

## Comparing the register

The displayed register increase is

\[
12.052-12.000=0.052\ \text{kWh}.
\]

Each true cumulative reading can differ from its display by up to 0.001 kWh. The difference can therefore differ from the displayed increase by up to the **sum** of those bounds:

\[
\Delta E_{\text{true}}
\in[0.052-0.002,\ 0.052+0.002]
=\boxed{[0.050,\ 0.054]\ \text{kWh}}.
\]

The analog result, 0.050 kWh, lies exactly at the lower boundary. For example, true register readings of 12.001 kWh initially and 12.051 kWh finally satisfy both display guarantees and give precisely that increase. The displayed discrepancy, 0.002 kWh or 2 Wh, is therefore compatible with the stated bounds. [A14-REGISTER]

This is a **possible-error range**, not a statistical confidence interval. No distribution or independence assumption permits assigning a probability to positions within it, assuming cancellation, or using a root-sum-of-squares calculation.

In a real installation, exact arithmetic would still be conditional on the measurement model. Approximate calibration, sensor drift, timing errors, or transitions between samples could make the analog total uncertain. Validate those assumptions and quantify their errors before interpreting a discrepancy or replacing an instrument. Compatibility here does not prove either instrument perfectly represents the physical load; it establishes that the supplied idealized calculation and register guarantees can both hold.
