The museum can reasonably accept that the diarist **did not hear a North Bell response at the south quay**. It should be substantially more cautious about saying the bell did not ring, and more cautious still about diagnosing a mechanical failure. The packet leaves both ringing without being heard and actual silence as live explanations.

### Three different claims

- **“The diarist did not hear it”** describes one person's listening experience at a particular place.
- **“The bell did not ring”** describes what happened at the bell, regardless of what anyone heard elsewhere.
- **“The mechanism could not ring”** adds a causal diagnosis: something physically prevented ringing.

The diary directly supports the first claim. Its dating to the storm night gives it clear relevance, but a warehouse ridge separates the listener from the North Bell, and normal audibility and wind effects at that location are unknown. Hearing the South Bell does not establish that the North Bell would also have been audible. Even genuine silence would not by itself distinguish a broken mechanism from a serviceable bell that nobody rang. [B22-S1]

### Three texts are not necessarily three witnesses

The year-45 chronicle lists the diary among its sources, so its stronger statement about silence might derive from that diary. Its lack of individual references prevents tracing this sentence with certainty.

The year-73 school history provides stronger signs of dependence on the chronicle: it shares an unusual phrase, repeats the same spelling error, and names the chronicle in its bibliography. These are reasons not to count the texts as three independent confirmations. They do not prove the exact origin of every sentence or rule out all additional information. [B22-S2]

The later works remain useful for other questions. They document how later writers presented the storm, including the use of bell failure as a symbol of absent leadership. They can support an account of later historical storytelling without independently confirming the original mechanism's condition. Dependence changes what a source can establish; it does not make the source worthless.

### Explanations still consistent with the evidence

| Explanation | What fits | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The North Bell rang, but its sound did not reach the diarist.** | The diary's location-specific wording, the intervening ridge, and strong wind allow this reading. The reconstruction shows that missed hearing is physically conceivable. | No supplied witness directly reports North Bell ringing that night. The test cannot reproduce the historical conditions closely enough to assign a probability. |
| **The North Bell did not ring.** | The diary is compatible with silence, and later histories explicitly describe silence, though they may be dependent. | The packet does not establish why it was silent. Mechanical failure and failure to operate a working bell remain different possibilities. |

A broken rope during the storm is one possible mechanical explanation. The later invoice for a new rope is consistent with it, but the same invoice also fits a rope that failed later or was replaced preventively. It neither dates a failure to the storm nor identifies a unique cause. [B22-S3]

The independent weather log confirms strong wind and flooding, not bell failure. The reconstructed-bell test strengthens the plausibility of an audibility problem, but differences in height and material prevent treating its results as calibrated odds for year 27. [B22-S3]

### Evidence that would separate the explanations

An independent, contemporary tower keeper's record describing an attempted ringing during the relevant watches would be especially useful. A report of actual ringing would favor the missed-hearing explanation; an attempted ringing followed by inspection of a broken component would favor mechanical failure. A record showing that no attempt was made would support a different cause of silence. Merely finding another later repetition of the same story would add much less.

A defensible museum caption would be:

> A diary from the storm night records that no North Bell response reached the south quay. Later histories describe the bell as silent, but may draw on earlier accounts. Whether the bell rang—and, if it did not, why—remains uncertain.

No exact percentage is justified. Multiplying assumed witness accuracies would require evidence about source dependence and how likely these observations would be under the competing explanations, neither of which is supplied. The festival's popularity adds nothing to the mechanical question. [B22-S4]
