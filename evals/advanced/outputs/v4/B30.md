The certified totals force **at least 100 and at most 500 learners to have both flags**. They do not determine whether the overlap exceeds 300, so they cannot settle the formal opening decision. The missing cross-tabulation limits the answer without making all deductions impossible.

## Deriving the bounds

Let \(x\) be the number with both flags. Then the whole population can be written as:

| Category | Number of learners |
| --- | ---: |
| Both flags | \(x\) |
| A only | \(600-x\) |
| B only | \(500-x\) |
| Neither | \(x-100\) |

Adding the flag totals gives 1,100 **flag memberships**, not 1,100 different people: someone with both flags appears in both totals. The number of distinct learners with at least one flag is \(600+500-x=1{,}100-x\). Because that cannot exceed the population of 1,000, \(x\geq100\).

The overlap also cannot exceed either flag total, so \(x\leq\min(600,500)=500\). All four category counts are nonnegative throughout \(100\leq x\leq500\), and they sum to 1,000. Thus the bounds are attainable, not merely loose limits (B30-S1).

## Two populations consistent with the same totals

| Category | Population 1 | Population 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Both flags | 100 | 500 |
| A only | 500 | 100 |
| B only | 400 | 0 |
| Neither | 0 | 400 |
| **Total population** | **1,000** | **1,000** |
| **Total with A** | **600** | **600** |
| **Total with B** | **500** | **500** |
| **More than 300 with both?** | **No** | **Yes** |

Both populations satisfy every certified total, yet they give opposite answers to the formal trigger. Unavoidable overlap therefore does not imply enough overlap to require a center.

The boundary is strict: **exactly 300 does not trigger opening**. That overlap is also feasible—for example, 300 with both, 300 with A only, 200 with B only, and 200 with neither. The informal “three hundred eligible learners are enough” summary neither amends the rule nor clearly identifies its eligible group. The stated criterion remains more than 300 learners with both flags (B30-S2).

## Why estimates and simulations cannot replace the missing fact

Multiplying \(0.6\times0.5\) gives 0.3, or 300 learners, only by adding an independence assumption: having one flag would not change the proportion expected to have the other. Nothing in the certified marginal totals establishes that relationship.

Generating ten thousand independent artificial datasets explores the analyst's assumed model. More simulations can make its average more stable, but cannot show that independence describes this actual population. Both populations in the table remain compatible with the evidence. The obstacle is missing information about how the flags coincide, not too few simulated datasets (B30-S3).

The discussion group's 80 reports out of 100 respondents do not repair the gap. Recruitment through an intensive-support channel and voluntary participation provide no basis for treating respondents as representative. Their flags were also unverified and unlinked. Applying their 80% directly to the population would imply 800 learners with both flags, exceeding even the certified maximum of 500. High overall satisfaction supplies no overlap measurement either (B30-S4).

## The minimum information needed for this decision

The minimum sufficient additional aggregate information is a trustworthy answer to one question:

**Do at least 301 of these same 1,000 learners have both administrative flags on the specified date?**

An authorized data holder could return that yes/no indicator. An exact overlap count would also suffice, but is more information than this particular decision requires. A certified lower bound of at least 301 would settle “yes”; a certified upper bound of at most 300 would settle “no.”

Calculating the indicator requires the data holder to assess the flags jointly, but the administrator need not receive names, linked identifiers, or individual records. The operational need is permission to obtain a reliable aggregate threshold result. If no such result can be provided, the available evidence supports the bounds, while leaving the opening trigger unresolved.
