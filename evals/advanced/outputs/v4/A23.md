The supplied numbers give a **12-point descriptive difference-in-differences** and a **6-point residual after a particular linear trend adjustment**. Neither establishes the tool's causal effect, because North was already improving faster and received additional staff at the same time as the tool.

## The specified descriptive calculation

Use equal weights for the two weeks in each period, as requested. These are averages of weekly percentages, not ticket-count-weighted pooled rates; ticket counts are unavailable. [A23-ANALYSIS]

| Region | Before: weeks −2 and −1 | After: weeks 0 and 1 | After minus before |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| North | `(84 + 88) / 2 = 86` | `(100 + 104) / 2 = 102` | **16 points** |
| South | `(51 + 52) / 2 = 51.5` | `(55 + 56) / 2 = 55.5` | **4 points** |

The difference-in-differences subtracts the comparison region's change from North's change:

\[
(102-86)-(55.5-51.5)=16-4=\boxed{12\text{ points}}.
\]

Equivalently, the North-minus-South gap rises from `86 − 51.5 = 34.5` before to `102 − 55.5 = 46.5` after, a 12-point increase. If the input rates were valid, these would be **percentage-point** differences, not relative percentage changes.

## What would make that contrast causal?

The essential assumption concerns an unobserved outcome: **without the intervention being evaluated, North would have experienced the same before-to-after change as South**. This is the parallel-trends assumption. It supplies the missing comparison for what would have happened to North without treatment.

The two regions do not need equal baseline levels. A persistent level difference is removed when changes are compared. A changing North–South gap, however, is not removed just by subtracting the baseline gap.

Here, the observed pre-period changes are:

| Pre-period transition | North change | South change | Difference in changes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Week −3 to −2 | +4 | +1 | +3 |
| Week −2 to −1 | +4 | +1 | +3 |

The regional gap grows from 30 to 33 to 36 points before rollout. That pattern gives a concrete reason to question the unadjusted parallel-trends assumption. It does not prove that those exact slopes would have continued: two observed pre-period transitions cannot determine the future untreated trajectory with certainty.

North's 16-point increase therefore cannot all be attributed to the tool. Subtracting South's 4-point increase removes South's observed change; it does not prove that South's change equals North's missing no-tool change. [A23-CONTEXT; A23-CLAIMS]

## Where the six-point adjusted result comes from

Suppose, as an additional modeling choice, that the observed pre-period linear trends would have continued: North gains 4 points per week and South gains 1 point per week.

Starting from week −1, the literal projections are:

| Region | Projected week 0 | Projected week 1 | Projected after average | Supplied after average minus projection |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| North | 92 | 96 | 94 | `102 − 94 = 8` |
| South | 53 | 54 | 53.5 | `55.5 − 53.5 = 2` |

The difference between those departures from projection is `8 − 2 = 6` points.

The same calculation can be made directly from the 12-point contrast. The before period is centered at week −1.5 and the after period at week 0.5, a **two-week** separation. A 3-point-per-week trend difference therefore predicts a 6-point difference between the period changes:

\[
12-(3\times2)=\boxed{6\text{ points}}.
\]

Subtracting just 3 points would incorrectly treat the comparison as one week apart.

This adjusted result depends on the assumed continuation of linear trends and on the absence of other unexplained differential changes. It is an assumption-dependent residual, not an automatically causal answer. Changing case mix, a different untreated trajectory, or region-specific events could change its interpretation. The historical pattern supports examining that model; it does not validate the model for the unobserved future.

## Staffing and the inconclusive pretrend test

The five experienced staff arrived in North at the same time as the tool, while South received neither intervention. The table contains no variation that separates their contributions. Even if a credible comparison trend established an effect of the North rollout package, it would not by itself divide that effect into tool, staffing, and any benefit from using them together. Measuring staffing would document the coincidence but would not solve this exact overlap in the present two-center comparison. [A23-CONTEXT]

For a tool-only interpretation, the missing no-tool comparison would need to represent North under the relevant staffing conditions. South, without North's staffing increase, is not established as that comparison.

A nonsignificant pretrend test does not prove parallel trends. It means the particular test did not reject its stated null under its assumptions and decision threshold. The test's model, uncertainty, and sample size were not retained, so its ability to detect a meaningful trend difference cannot be assessed here. It would be unjustified either to declare the trend difference statistically significant from this table or to declare it absent because of the reported test. Even a well-documented pretrend test cannot directly observe the post-rollout untreated outcome. [A23-CLAIMS]

## A follow-up that informs the expansion decision

Use additional comparable centers and randomize **when the tool starts**, comparing centers assigned to start now with centers assigned to start later during the same calendar periods. For the primary tool-only comparison, maintain the same staffing policy across those groups and avoid tying staff increases to tool assignment. Randomize at the center level because the intervention changes the center's routing operation; individual tickets within a center share that environment.

To identify the separate staffing contribution and the combined package as well, arrange independent random assignment of staffing and tool rollout if operationally possible:

| Group | Tool | Additional staff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usual practice during comparison period | No | No |
| Tool only | Yes | No |
| Staffing only | No | Yes |
| Combined package | Yes | Yes |

The tool-only versus usual-practice comparison estimates the tool's effect without added staff. The combined versus usual-practice comparison estimates the package's effect. The combined versus tool-only comparison estimates the benefit of adding staff when the tool is present. Including staffing-only also reveals whether the two interventions work differently together than their separate effects would suggest.

Independent staffing assignment is an additional design requirement, not a capability established by the packet. If only tool timing can be randomized, a trial with staffing held unchanged can still identify a tool-only effect. Recording nonrandom staffing changes alone would not establish the staffing or combined-package effect with the same credibility.

Collect ticket-level outcomes and counts, staffing levels, case mix, assignment and activation dates, and sufficient pre- and post-period observations. Prespecify the outcome, weighting, comparison periods, and how uncertainty will account for center-level assignment and repeated observations. Check that comparison centers do not indirectly receive the intervention, for example through shared routing or staff. The completed North rollout can remain useful operational context; it need not be undone to run the future study. [A23-DECISION]

The percentage inconsistency needs correction, and the packet does not identify a tool-only causal effect or support a numerical standard error, confidence interval, or significance claim.
