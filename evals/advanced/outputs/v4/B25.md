## Nominal schedules

All intervals below are hours after the start. Calibration uses the scanner and immediately precedes the same job's scan, as required by B25-S1.

| Scanner order | Job | Calibration | Scan | OCR | Review | Completion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, then B | A | 0–1 | 1–5 | 5–11 | 11–13 | 13 |
| A, then B | B | 5–6 | 6–8 | 8–11 | 13–16 | 16 |
| B, then A | B | 0–1 | 1–3 | 3–6 | 6–9 | 9 |
| B, then A | A | 3–4 | 4–8 | 8–14 | 14–16 | 16 |

With **A first**, both OCR jobs finish at hour 11. The single reviewer must choose an order. Reviewing A from 11 to 13 lets it meet its deadline; B then finishes exactly at its deadline of 16. If the reviewer instead takes B from 11 to 14, A must wait and finishes at 16, three hours late. The final project completion remains 16 either way.

With **B first**, B finishes much earlier, at hour 9, but A cannot finish before hour 16: its scan ends at 8, OCR takes another six hours, and review takes two. An earlier first completion therefore does not establish better deadline performance. Both scanner orders can finish the overall project at hour 16, while only the first schedule above meets both individual deadlines. These conclusions use the exact nominal durations in B25-S2.

The manager's 22 hours is the sum of all stage durations: eight scanner-hours, nine OCR-hours, and five reviewer-hours. It measures accumulated processing time across resources. Elapsed time is shorter because different resources work simultaneously; it also should not be described as 22 hours of human effort.

## What the proposed changes achieve

**An additional OCR computer:** There is no shortage of simultaneous OCR capacity in this model, and the computer does not shorten either OCR runtime. It changes neither schedule. The constraints that matter here are the scanner sequence, each job's required sequence of stages, and the single reviewer when both jobs become ready together.

**Expediting A's OCR:** With A first, A's OCR runs from 5 to 9 and its review from 9 to 11. B's OCR still runs from 8 to 11, followed by review from 11 to 14. A finishes at 11 and B at 14, giving each two hours of nominal deadline margin. With B first, A's expedited OCR runs from 8 to 12 and review from 12 to 14, so A still misses its deadline by one hour.

The service therefore offers a concrete scheduling improvement for 100 currency units. Whether that improvement is worth its price requires values for earlier completion, avoided lateness, or other relevant costs; none are supplied in B25-S3.

## What can be guaranteed

Let \(d\) be A's actual ordinary OCR duration. Even with A starting immediately and never waiting for a resource, its earliest completion is

\[
1+4+d+2=7+d\text{ hours}.
\]

Meeting hour 13 requires \(d\leq6\). The supplied range allows \(d=9\), when even this earliest possible path finishes at hour 16. Thus the ordinary process cannot guarantee A's deadline across that range, regardless of scanner or review scheduling.

No probability of lateness follows from a range without a probability distribution. Excellent cluster uptime does not bound this file's processing time, and its layout complexity remains unexamined. The expedited service's four-hour quotation likewise supports the nominal calculation, but provides no worst-case completion guarantee (B25-S4).
