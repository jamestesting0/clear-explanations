**J1 executes two failed attempts and expires at time 6 before its third attempt starts.** Its third attempt becomes eligible at 6, but would finish at 7, beyond its deadline of 6. The three-attempt limit permits a third attempt; it does not guarantee that the deadline will allow it.

## Actual worker timeline

The queue sorts by eligibility time first, then job identifier, then attempt number. Eligibility is the earliest allowed start time, not a reserved worker slot. Every executed attempt occupies the worker for one second. [A22-SCHEDULER]

| Worker event | Eligibility time | Actual start | Completion | Result and subsequent scheduling |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| J1, attempt 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | Failure; attempt 2 becomes eligible at `1 + 1 = 2`. |
| J2, attempt 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | Failure; attempt 2 becomes eligible at `2 + 1 = 3`. |
| J3, attempt 1 | 0 | 2 | 3 | Success; J3 ends. |
| J1, attempt 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Failure; attempt 3 becomes eligible at `4 + 2 = 6`. |
| J2, attempt 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Success; J2 ends. |
| Worker idle | — | 5 | 6 | Nothing is eligible; the next scheduled time is 6. |
| J1, attempt 3 selected | 6 | **Never starts** | **No completion** | At time 6, proposed completion `6 + 1 = 7` exceeds deadline 6; J1 expires immediately. |

At time 0, all eligibility times tie, so lexical order selects J1, then J2 when the worker becomes free at 1. At time 2, J3's initial attempt still has eligibility time 0, which precedes J1's retry eligibility time 2. J3 therefore runs first. At time 3, J1's retry, eligible since 2, precedes J2's retry, eligible at 3.

The deadline checks permit every attempt that actually runs: J1's completions at 1 and 4 are within deadline 6; J2's completions at 2 and 5 are within deadline 10; J3 has no deadline. J1's third attempt is enqueued at time 4 even though its earliest possible completion would be too late. The specification requires the expiration check at selection time, so its expiration occurs at **6**, not 4. Expiration itself consumes no attempt execution time. A completion exactly at 6 would be allowed, but this attempt cannot start before 6. [A22-DEADLINE]

The only idle interval before all jobs terminate is **[5, 6)**. Five attempts execute in total, consuming five seconds of worker time.

## Realized outcomes and the incident claims

| Job | Outcomes actually realized | Executed attempts | Final state |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| J1 | Failure, failure | 2 | Expired; the listed third-attempt success remains hypothetical. |
| J2 | Failure, success | 2 | Succeeded. |
| J3 | Success | 1 | Succeeded. |

All three incident claims are incorrect:

1. **Backoff does not set J1's retries at absolute times 1 and 2.** Those numbers are the first and second delay durations. Each delay starts after the corresponding failure completes. J1's first failure completes at 1, giving retry eligibility 2. Its second failure completes at 4, giving retry eligibility 6. Queue waiting affects when a failure completes and therefore when its next retry becomes eligible. [A22-RETRY]
2. **An old job's retry does not outrank older eligible work.** At time 2, J3's attempt has eligibility 0 and J1's retry has eligibility 2. The specified queue order selects J3; there is no separate retry priority.
3. **The attempt limit does not guarantee three tries or eventual success.** Three is the maximum number of executed attempts, including the initial attempt. Success can end a job earlier, and the absolute deadline can prevent a still-permitted attempt from starting. An outcome listed for an unexecuted attempt is not an observed success.

The attempt limit controls how many executions may occur. The deadline controls how late an execution may finish in absolute time. A job's elapsed lifetime includes its own execution, deliberate retry delays, and waiting for other eligible work. Those are separate constraints and sources of delay.

## Production handling and instrumentation

Treat expiration as a distinct terminal outcome. Record the job, executed-attempt count, deadline, last failure, and expiration reason; preserve enough information for investigation or a configured recovery process. Depending on the job's purpose, that process might notify its owner, retain it in a failed-job queue, or permit a new submission under an explicit fresh deadline. It should not label the original job successful.

Removing the deadline check changes the service's contract and could allow work whose result is no longer useful or acceptable. Whether that is appropriate depends on why the deadline exists. If recovery can repeat side effects from a prior failed attempt, the operation also needs duplicate protection so that rerunning it does not perform the same external action twice. Permanent invalid-input failures would need a separate stop-or-correct policy; the exercise's failures are all retryable.

For each attempt, record the job identifier, attempt number, initial enqueue time, scheduled eligibility time, selection time, actual start and completion times, outcome, and any next retry eligibility. Also record deadlines and rejected starts separately from executed attempts. These timestamps distinguish:

- **Retry delay:** next eligibility minus the previous failure's completion. J1 has delays of 1 and 2 seconds; J2 has one delay of 1 second.
- **Queue waiting after eligibility:** actual start minus eligibility. For the five executed attempts in timeline order, this is 0, 1, 2, 1, and 1 seconds.
- **Execution time:** completion minus actual start, exactly one second for each executed attempt here.
- **Total job lifetime:** initial enqueue to success or expiration. Here that is 6 seconds for J1, 5 for J2, and 3 for J3.

J1's expired third attempt has a selection and expiration event, but no start, completion, or execution duration. Counting it as an executed attempt would obscure the incident.

In a deployment with many workers, random variation in retry delays—jitter—can spread out retries that would otherwise hit a recovering dependency simultaneously after a shared failure. This exercise explicitly has no jitter, so it does not change any time in the deterministic timeline above.
