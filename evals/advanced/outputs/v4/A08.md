The window emits **12**, then a replacement total of **15**. Its state is deleted at step 7. The amount **4** at step 8 is dropped and counted; reactivating its partition neither moves the watermark backward nor restores the deleted window.

Here, the watermark is the protocol's progress marker for deciding when to emit and stop accepting older data. It is computed from observed event timestamps and partition status, not from the wall clock. [A08-RULES]

## Step-by-step trace

Each active partition proposes its maximum observed timestamp minus 10. The global watermark uses the smallest active proposal, but never decreases. Initially both maxima are zero and the watermark is `−10`.

For a data message, acceptance uses the watermark **before** that message updates the partition maximum or reactivates an idle partition.

| Step | Partition maxima after the step | Global watermark, before → after | Fate of `[0,60)` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: p0 data at 20, amount 5 | p0: 20; p1: 0 | `−10 → −10` | Accept 5; total 5; no output |
| 2: p1 data at 50, amount 7 | p0: 20; p1: 50 | `−10 → 10` | Accept 7; total 12; no output |
| 3: p0 heartbeat at 80 | p0: 80; p1: 50 | `10 → 40` | Retain total 12; no output |
| 4: p1 heartbeat at 75 | p0: 80; p1: 75 | `40 → 65` | Emit initial total **12**; retain state |
| 5: p0 data at 40, amount 3 | p0: 80; p1: 75 | `65 → 65` | Accept 3; emit replacement total **15** |
| 6: p1 marked idle | p0: 80; p1: 75, idle | `65 → 70` | Retain total 15; no output |
| 7: p0 heartbeat at 100 | p0: 100; p1: 75, idle | `70 → 90` | Delete window state; no output |
| 8: p1 data at 55, amount 4 | p0: 100; p1: 75, active | `90 → 90` | Drop and count the record; state stays deleted |

At step 4, the active proposals become `80−10=70` and `75−10=65`; their minimum reaches 65. That crosses the initial-emission threshold of 60, so the window emits 12. The heartbeat adds no amount. Triggering this first emission is distinct from producing a correction, which controls do not do.

At step 5, the event timestamp 40 belongs to `[0,60)`. Although the initial total has already been emitted, the current watermark is 65, still below the acceptance cutoff of 80. The amount 3 is therefore accepted and the updated total 15 is emitted immediately. The timestamp 40 does not reduce p0's existing maximum of 80.

At step 6, excluding p1 removes its proposal of 65 from the minimum. The remaining proposal is p0's 70, so the watermark can advance. This is how marking an inactive partition idle prevents it from holding back other partitions indefinitely.

At step 7, p0's proposal becomes `100−10=90`. The watermark crosses 80, permanently deleting the window state. There is no additional emission under the supplied rules.

At step 8, acceptance is judged against the existing watermark of 90, so the record is dropped. It still reactivates p1. Since maxima never decrease, p1's maximum remains 75, giving a proposal of 65. The recomputed minimum would be 65, but the monotonic rule keeps the global watermark at 90. Reactivation makes p1 participate in subsequent progress calculations; it cannot reopen this window.

Thus, the four supplied amounts belonging to the minute sum to 19, but the policy accepts only 15 into the emitted result. One record, containing amount 4, is counted as dropped. There are no further messages to process. [A08-INPUT]

## The downstream consumer must replace totals

Adding the two emitted numbers produces `12 + 15 = 27`, which is incorrect. The second message already includes the first 12. Redelivery would make addition overcount again. A window identifier and total alone also cannot reliably distinguish an older replacement arriving late from the current replacement. [A08-DOWNSTREAM]

A safe output contract would include:

- A stable **window identifier**, including the aggregation key where applicable and the window boundaries.
- A monotonically increasing **version for that window**, such as version 1 with total 12 and version 2 with total 15.
- A stable message identity, conveniently `(window identifier, version)`, reused when that same output is redelivered. One version must never identify two different totals.

The consumer atomically compares the incoming version with its stored version for the window. It replaces the stored total when the version is newer, ignores an identical duplicate, and ignores an older version. The same version with a different total is a conflict to investigate. If it maintains a grand total across windows, it can atomically add `incoming total − previously stored window total` while updating the window's version and total.

The producer must preserve those identities across retries and restarts, and the consumer must retain its version information for as long as old messages can return. Otherwise, forgetting an identity can make an old message look new. With those semantics, any order or repetition of these two outputs leaves the consumer with 15.

## What “final” means under this policy

The first emission is preliminary because updates remain permitted while the watermark is below 80. State deletion ends updates **under this policy**; it does not prove that no other event for the minute can exist or arrive.

The chosen cutoff trades the ability to incorporate arbitrarily late data for earlier progress and the ability to release window state once the watermark advances. Excluding idle partitions helps make that progress possible, while exposing returning partitions to dropped data. Keeping every window forever would preserve the possibility of incorporating arbitrarily late arrivals, but would remove this finite cleanup rule and still would not prove that a missing event will ever arrive. Neither crossing the wall-clock minute nor deleting state establishes a physical-world completeness guarantee.
