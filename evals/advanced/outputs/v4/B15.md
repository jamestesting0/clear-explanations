Restoring one part of a process can change an intermediate condition without being sufficient to restore the final outcome. Here, the results fit a model in which colonies need **both adequate light and adequate mineral M**: fixing either shortage alone leaves the other in place.

## What the single and combined rescues show

The proposed shelter pathway is that fronds protect grazers, grazers remove film, and less film allows more light. Warming may also reduce M through a separate pathway. These remain proposed explanations, not a fully established network. [B15-S1]

| Condition | Light | M | Established colonies |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Untreated | 80 | 8 | 40 |
| Warmed | 20 | 2 | 5 |
| Warmed + grazers | 80 | 2 | 6 |
| Warmed + M | 20 | 8 | 7 |
| Warmed + both | 80 | 8 | 38 |

These are condition means, with four tanks per condition. [B15-S2]

Adding grazers restores their mean abundance from 3 to 10, reduces film from 7 to 1, and restores measured light from 20 to 80. That is substantial change in the intermediate conditions, even though colonies rise only from 5 to 6. Under the proposed model, low M still blocks establishment. Thus, the small final response does not show that grazers or film are irrelevant. [B15-S2]

Adding M alone restores its measured concentration to 8, but light remains 20 and colonies remain low at 7. Adding both restores the two proposed requirements together, and the colony mean reaches 38. This is consistent with complementary requirements: the benefit of satisfying one becomes much larger when the other is already satisfied. It does not prove that these are the only mechanisms operating.

## Why 38 is compatible with small individual improvements

The additive prediction assumes each addition has the same effect regardless of whether the other is present:

**5 + (6 − 5) + (7 − 5) = 8 colonies.**

But that assumption need not hold. With low M, grazer addition changes the mean by only 1 colony. With M added, grazer addition changes it by **38 − 7 = 31**. Likewise, adding M changes the mean by 2 without added grazers and by **38 − 6 = 32** with them. [Calculations from B15-S2]

The combined mean is therefore 30 colonies above the additive prediction. This is an **interaction**: the effect of one intervention depends on the other intervention. A process requiring both light and M can produce exactly this kind of pattern. The point estimates show a large interaction, but the packet lacks the individual observations and uncertainty estimates needed to assess its precision. Nor does 38 versus 40 establish complete recovery or equivalence to untreated tanks.

## Which parts of the mechanism have support?

- **Assigned treatments and measured outcomes:** Random assignment supports causal comparisons of the warming and addition treatments in these tanks. Grazer addition has experimental support for changing film and light; the combined intervention has support for a much larger mean colony response than either single addition. Sampling variation still matters. [B15-S2]
- **Fronds and shelter:** Adding grazers bypasses shelter rather than restoring it. The main experiment therefore does not establish that warming reduced fronds, that frond loss caused the grazer decline, or that frond loss was the sole cause of damage. The shelter pilot is suggestive, but its added shade complicates interpretation and it measured no colony outcome. [B15-S1, B15-S3]
- **Film, light, and establishment:** Film and light change together after grazer addition. Their separate causal roles are not isolated because light was not independently manipulated. The filmed grazer establishes that film eating can occur; it does not measure how much that behavior explains the tank-wide changes. [B15-S3]
- **M and alternative pathways:** M supplementation changes the measured mineral level, but its effect cannot automatically be attributed solely to M if the addition has other effects. Similarly, warming's effect on measured M does not by itself establish that the route is direct rather than through an unmeasured intermediate. [B15-S1–S3]

## Two focused follow-up comparisons

1. **Manipulate light and M independently in warmed tanks.** Hold grazers and film comparable, then cross low versus restored light with low versus restored M, beginning before establishment. Compare colony counts across the four combinations. This tests whether light and M themselves have complementary effects without relying on grazer addition to change light. A matched carrier control and an independent method of changing M would help distinguish mineral effects from effects of the supplement's other ingredients.

2. **Compare usable shelter with a control that casts the same shade but offers no refuge.** Keep warming and M comparable, and measure grazer survival or abundance, film, light at the colony surface, and establishment. This tests whether shelter supports grazers independently of shading, then whether that change carries through to later outcomes. It directly addresses the link bypassed by adding animals and the ambiguity in the earlier shelter pilot.

These comparisons would test specific remaining explanations. The present results support reasoning about the model tanks; they do not establish the full causal network or its applicability to real ponds.
