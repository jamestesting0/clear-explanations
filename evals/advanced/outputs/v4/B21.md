**The opening customer group lost seven accounts, even though its contracted monthly recurring revenue rose.** Expansion by four large accounts more than offset the recurring revenue lost through cancellations. That is what net recurring-revenue retention above 100% means here; it does not mean every customer stayed.

### Account and recurring-revenue totals

Monthly recurring revenue is the monthly amount represented by the contracts at a particular point in time. The closing figures are:

| Group | Closing accounts | Closing monthly recurring revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Surviving opening Starter accounts | 45 | 900 units/month |
| Surviving opening Pro accounts | 9 | 900 units/month |
| Surviving opening Enterprise accounts | 4 | 6,000 units/month |
| **Opening cohort at closing** | **58** | **7,800 units/month** |
| Newly acquired Starter accounts | 20 | 400 units/month |
| Newly acquired Pro accounts | 2 | 200 units/month |
| **All accounts at closing** | **80** | **8,400 units/month** |

A **cohort** is the fixed group being followed: here, the 65 accounts present at opening. The 22 new accounts belong in the company's closing totals, but not in retention calculations for that opening group. An existing account that expands remains the same customer. [B21-S1–S3]

### Three retention measures answer three questions

| Measure | Calculation | Result | What it answers |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Account retention | 58 ÷ 65 | **89.23%** | What fraction of opening customers remained? |
| Net recurring-revenue retention | 7,800 ÷ 7,000 | **111.43%** | How much recurring revenue did that same group retain, including expansion? |
| Gross recurring-revenue retention | 5,800 ÷ 7,000 | **82.86%** | How much opening recurring revenue remained when expansion is excluded? |

These percentages use the supplied definitions. [B21-S4]

The canceled accounts were five Starter, one Pro, and one Enterprise account. Their opening recurring revenue totaled:

\[
5(20) + 1(100) + 1(1{,}000) = 1{,}200\text{ units/month}.
\]

Meanwhile, the four surviving Enterprise accounts expanded from 4,000 to 6,000 units/month, adding 2,000. Therefore:

\[
7{,}000 - 1{,}200 + 2{,}000 = 7{,}800\text{ units/month}.
\]

Gross retention excludes that 2,000 expansion, leaving 5,800. Because no surviving account reduced its recurring revenue, all the gross loss in this example comes from complete cancellations. Large customers carry much more weight in revenue retention than in account retention, where every account counts once. [B21-S1–S2]

Dividing **all** closing recurring revenue by opening recurring revenue gives:

\[
8{,}400 \div 7{,}000 = 120\%.
\]

An honest label is **“closing total monthly recurring revenue as a percentage of opening monthly recurring revenue,”** or simply **“20% total monthly recurring-revenue growth.”** It combines the opening cohort's net increase of 800 with 600 from new customers. It answers a company-growth question, not a same-customer retention question.

### What this snapshot cannot establish

**Cash collection:** Contract amounts do not reveal how much cash arrived. Annual prepayments include money for future service, while overdue invoices relate to earlier periods. Obtain receipt amounts and match them to their invoices and service periods. [B21-S3]

**Revenue earned during the month:** Closing contract amounts are not a complete record of services provided throughout the month. Upgrade and cancellation dates, service periods, and applicable revenue-recognition records are needed; 8,400 units/month is a closing rate, not established earned revenue for this month. [B21-S2–S3]

**Profit:** Higher recurring revenue does not establish higher profit. The increased support workload may affect costs, but its financial effect is unquantified. Compare earned revenue with the corresponding cost ledger, including support and acquisition costs, before making a profit claim. [B21-S3–S4]

**Satisfaction and cancellations:** Seven opening accounts canceled. The packet does not establish that cancellations later stopped or explain why they occurred. A survey excluding canceled accounts cannot establish their reasons, and an unknown response rate limits conclusions even about active customers. Seek cancellation timing, feedback from departed customers, and survey invitation and response counts; praise from surviving respondents alone does not establish why customers stayed. [B21-S2–S4]

**Future durability:** One month's ending contracts cannot establish future renewals or continued expansion. Track the same measures across later cohorts and renewal periods, including dependence on the four large expanding accounts.
