Under the enacted rules supplied here, **Alder requires an inspection certificate; Birch qualifies to use a transition declaration, provided it is properly filed; Cedar needs a conditional decision because its place of first use and its customer-delivery facts are not established.** A newer publication date does not make a document legally controlling in this fictional scheme.

## Apply the documents in order of authority

The enacted ordinance sets scope and eligibility. The implementing circular can specify paperwork and filing arrangements, but cannot change the ordinance's eligibility dates. Its July 15 filing date therefore does not extend the physical-delivery cutoff. [B03-S1–S2]

The May 11 slide is an informal summary. Its phrase “orders received before April” omits the delivery condition and substitutes an undefined event for a signed agreement. Where it differs from the ordinance, use the ordinance. [B03-S2]

The May 28 amendment is a proposal requiring a separate council vote. The packet records no enactment or promulgation. The June 3 news digest links only to that draft, so its confident phrase “June extension” does not establish a change in the rules. The modernization brochure provides no rule at all. [B03-S3]

## Keep the different dates separate

| Date or event | Its role in the supplied rules |
| --- | --- |
| April 2 publication of the ordinance | The reference date for the purchase-agreement condition: a qualifying signed agreement must predate April 2 |
| June 1 commencement | Commercial equipment first placed into service within Port Meridian from this date falls under the certificate requirement |
| Physical customer delivery before June 1 | A separate condition for using a transition declaration instead of a certificate |
| First operation | Determines when equipment is placed into service; it is not the same as delivery or filing |
| July 15 | Last permitted filing date for a declaration whose substantive conditions were already met |
| June 15 in the draft | A proposed delivery cutoff, with no established effect in this packet |

For the transition, both the agreement and delivery conditions must hold. Dispatch, arrival at a transit warehouse, customs clearance, or an invoice cannot substitute for physical delivery to the customer. Filing may follow first use, but filing cannot fix an agreement or delivery that misses the eligibility conditions. [B03-S1–S2]

## Alder: certificate required under the supplied ordinance

Alder is commercial equipment first operated in Port Meridian on June 5, so the June 1 commencement rule applies. Its March 29 signed agreement meets the agreement condition. However, physical delivery to the customer occurred on June 3, after the delivery cutoff. Arrival at the port warehouse on May 29 does not replace the stated customer-delivery date. Alder therefore does not qualify for a transition declaration. [B03-S4]

The proposed extension does not change this conclusion. Even as a hypothetical, that draft concerns delays caused by dock repairs; the packet does not establish that cause for Alder. The team would need an actually enacted amendment and its final conditions before applying a different rule. [B03-S3–S4]

## Birch: eligible for the transition, with filing still to complete

Birch's April 1 signed agreement predates April 2, and its May 31 physical delivery to its Port Meridian commercial customer occurred before June 1. Both transition conditions are satisfied. Its first operation on June 2 does not disqualify it: the circular expressly allows filing after first use. [B03-S1–S2, B03-S4]

The July 10 appointment is before July 15, so the planned timing is permitted. An appointment is not evidence of a completed declaration, however. Birch must actually file the declaration by July 15 with the signed agreement, proof of physical customer delivery, and equipment serial number. On those conditions, it may use the declaration instead of an inspection certificate. The slide's “before April” wording would wrongly exclude Birch's April 1 agreement. [B03-S2, B03-S4]

## Cedar: resolve jurisdiction and customer delivery

Cedar's commercial use and March 20 signed agreement are established. Two other facts are missing.

**First, where is the workshop in which Cedar first operated on June 4?** The district map or an authoritative district assignment is needed. A warehouse postal address saying “Meridian Bay” does not establish the workshop's district. If first service was within Port Meridian, the ordinance applies. If it was in Estuary, this ordinance does not apply, but the packet gives no basis for deciding what Estuary requires. Being outside Port Meridian does not establish exemption from every possible regime. [B03-S1, B03-S4]

**Second, did the May 25 warehouse delivery constitute physical delivery to the customer?** The packet says only that the equipment reached a warehouse. If this was delivery to the customer, or other evidence establishes physical customer delivery before June 1, Cedar meets the substantive transition conditions. It could then use a declaration if the required documents and filing deadline are met. No filing information for Cedar is supplied. [B03-S2, B03-S4]

If the warehouse was only a transit or seller-controlled storage point, the actual customer-delivery date is needed. Delivery before June 1 would satisfy that condition; delivery on or after June 1 would not. If Cedar first operated within Port Meridian and timely customer delivery cannot be established, the packet does not support approving it for the transition route; the applicable default is an inspection certificate. [B03-S1–S2]

The team's decision process should therefore be: establish where and when commercial service began, apply the enacted rule, check the signed-agreement and physical-customer-delivery conditions separately, and then check the declaration paperwork and filing date. Publication dates identify documents; their authority and content determine which rules apply.
